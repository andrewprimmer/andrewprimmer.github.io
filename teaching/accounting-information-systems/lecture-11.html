Microteach 11.1 · 1:16The fraud triangle

Criminologist Donald Cressey's fraud triangle says fraud usually needs three things.

Read the script

Why do honest people commit fraud? Three conditions usually come together.

Criminologist Donald Cressey's fraud triangle says fraud usually needs three things.

Pressure: a financial or emotional push, like debts, addiction, or targets that must be hit.

Opportunity: the chance to commit the fraud and hide it, usually because of weak controls.

Rationalization: a story that makes it feel acceptable. I'm only borrowing it. They underpay me anyway.

Accountants can't do much about pressure or rationalization. But internal controls directly reduce opportunity. That's our job.

There are two main kinds of fraud. Misappropriation of assets: stealing cash, inventory or time. And fraudulent financial reporting: deliberately misstating the financial statements, as at Enron and WorldCom.

So: pressure, opportunity, rationalization. And our job is to remove the opportunity.

Quick check. Which side of the fraud triangle do internal controls reduce most? Pause and pick.

Opportunity.

Microteach 11.2 · 1:27SOX Sections 302 and 404

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act, or SOX, was passed in two thousand two after a wave of accounting scandals.

Read the script

After Enron and WorldCom, Congress made executives personally sign off on their numbers.

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act, or SOX, was passed in two thousand two after a wave of accounting scandals. It applies to US public companies.

Section three oh two requires the CEO and CFO to certify each quarterly and annual report, including that they're responsible for internal controls.

Section four oh four requires management to assess internal control over financial reporting every year. For larger public companies, the external auditor must also give an opinion on it.

SOX also created the PCAOB, the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, which sets auditing standards for public company audits and inspects the audit firms.

For the AIS, SOX means documented processes, tested IT controls, segregation of duties built into ERP roles, and evidence for every key control.

So: three oh two, certify. Four oh four, assess and audit internal control.

Quick check. Which SOX section requires an assessment of internal control over financial reporting? Pause and pick.

Section four oh four.

Microteach 11.3 · 1:18COSO Internal Control framework

COSO is the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission.

Read the script

When a US company says its controls are effective, it almost always means effective under COSO.

COSO is the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission. Its Internal Control Integrated Framework, updated in twenty thirteen, is the standard used for SOX assessments.

It has five components: the control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring activities.

The control environment is the foundation: tone at the top, ethics, board oversight and accountability. Without it, the other controls don't hold.

The twenty thirteen framework adds seventeen principles spread across the five components. All must be present and functioning for controls to be effective.

A handy memory aid is crime: control activities, risk assessment, information and communication, monitoring, and the control environment.

So COSO gives us five components and seventeen principles.

Quick check. Tone at the top belongs to which COSO component? Pause and pick.

The control environment.

Microteach 11.4 · 1:14COSO ERM

COSO's Enterprise Risk Management framework, updated in twenty seventeen, links risk to strategy and performance across the whole organization.

Read the script

Internal control protects the financial statements. Enterprise risk management protects the whole strategy.

COSO's Enterprise Risk Management framework, updated in twenty seventeen, links risk to strategy and performance across the whole organization.

It has five components: governance and culture; strategy and objective-setting; performance; review and revision; and information, communication and reporting.

A central idea is risk appetite: how much risk the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its goals.

There are four responses to a risk. Accept it. Avoid it, by not doing the activity. Reduce it with controls. Or share it, for example through insurance or outsourcing.

Internal controls are the reduce response. And a control is only worth having if its benefit exceeds its cost.

So ERM asks: what risks threaten our strategy, and how will we respond?

Quick check. Buying insurance is which risk response? Pause and pick.

Share.

Microteach 11.5 · 1:10Preventive, detective and corrective controls

Controls come in three types.

Read the script

A lock, an alarm and an insurance claim. Three different kinds of control.

Controls come in three types. Preventive controls stop problems before they happen. Detective controls find them after they happen. Corrective controls fix the damage.

Preventive examples in an ERP: passwords, required approvals before posting, segregation of duties, and edit checks that reject invalid data.

Detective examples: bank reconciliations, management review of reports, exception reports, and audit logs.

Corrective examples: restoring data from backups, correcting and resubmitting rejected transactions, and patching a security flaw that was exploited.

Prevention is usually cheaper than detection, but no preventive control is perfect, so a good system layers all three.

So: stop it, find it, fix it.

Quick check. A monthly bank reconciliation is which type of control? Pause and pick.

A detective control.

Microteach 11.6 · 1:12Segregation of duties in an ERP

Segregation of duties separates three functions: authorizing transactions, recording them, and having custody of the assets.

Read the script

If one person can do everything, one person can steal everything.

Segregation of duties separates three functions: authorizing transactions, recording them, and having custody of the assets.

Example: one person approves the invoice, another records it in accounts payable, and a third releases the payment.

In an ERP, segregation is built into roles. Each role has a set of permissions, and users are given roles, not individual permissions.

An SoD conflict happens when one user holds incompatible permissions, like creating a vendor and paying that vendor. Tools scan for these conflicts automatically.

Small companies can't always separate duties. They compensate with mandatory vacations, the owner reviewing payments and bank statements, and detailed audit logs.

So: authorization, recording and custody, in different hands.

Quick check. A user who can both create vendors and pay them has what? Pause and pick.

A segregation of duties conflict.
