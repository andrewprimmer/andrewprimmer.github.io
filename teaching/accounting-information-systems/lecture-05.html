Microteach 5.1 · 1:32SAP S/4HANA

SAP was founded in nineteen seventy-two in Germany by five former IBM engineers.

Read the script

Walk into most large multinationals and you'll find SAP running the books.

SAP was founded in nineteen seventy-two in Germany by five former IBM engineers. It's the largest ERP vendor for big enterprises.

Its flagship is S/4HANA. The S/4 marks the fourth generation of the suite, and HANA is SAP's in-memory database, which keeps data in memory so reports run in real time.

Key features: the in-memory database; the universal journal, which keeps finance and controlling data in one table; the Fiori web interface; and both cloud and on-premise editions.

You'll see the classic two-letter module names in job ads: FI for financial accounting, CO for controlling, SD for sales and distribution, MM for materials management, and PP for production planning.

Mainstream maintenance for SAP's older ECC system ends in twenty twenty-seven, so many companies are moving to S/4HANA right now. That means strong demand for people with SAP skills.

So SAP S/4HANA is the go-to system for large, complex, global companies.

Quick check. In S/4HANA, what is HANA? Pause and pick.

SAP's in-memory database.

Microteach 5.2 · 1:24Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP and Oracle NetSuite

Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP is Oracle's suite for large enterprises.

Read the script

Oracle sells two very different ERP systems. Here's who uses which.

Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP is Oracle's suite for large enterprises. It's cloud-native software as a service, covering financials, procurement, projects and risk management.

It's updated every quarter, includes built-in AI features, and is especially strong in financials and in planning and consolidation.

Oracle NetSuite is different. It was one of the first cloud ERPs, launched in the late nineteen nineties, and Oracle bought it in twenty sixteen. It targets small and mid-sized, fast-growing companies.

NetSuite combines ERP, CRM and e-commerce in one system. It's relatively quick to implement, popular with startups and scale-ups, and it handles multiple subsidiaries well.

A common path: a company outgrows QuickBooks and moves to NetSuite. Much bigger companies tend to choose Fusion or SAP.

So: Fusion for the enterprise, NetSuite for the mid-market.

Quick check. A fast-growing startup that's outgrown QuickBooks would most likely move to which system? Pause and pick.

Oracle NetSuite.

Microteach 5.3 · 1:20Microsoft Dynamics 365

Microsoft Dynamics 365 is Microsoft's family of cloud business applications, covering both ERP and CRM.

Read the script

If your company lives in Outlook, Excel and Teams, there's a good chance its ERP is Microsoft too.

Microsoft Dynamics 365 is Microsoft's family of cloud business applications, covering both ERP and CRM.

There are two ERP products. Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management targets larger and international companies. Business Central targets small and mid-sized businesses.

Companies choose it because it works smoothly with Excel, Outlook and Teams, reports through Power BI, includes Microsoft's Copilot AI assistant, and is supported by a large network of partners.

Microsoft mostly sells Dynamics through partners, who configure it, migrate data and provide support. Those partners hire plenty of accounting graduates who know systems.

So Dynamics 365 is the ERP with the familiar Microsoft feel, from small business to enterprise.

Quick check. Which Dynamics product is aimed at small and mid-sized businesses? Pause and pick.

Business Central.

Microteach 5.4 · 1:11Workday

Workday was founded in twenty oh five by Dave Duffield and Aneel Bhusri, after Oracle took over Duffield's company, PeopleSoft.

Read the script

Many universities and big employers pay their staff through Workday.

Workday was founded in twenty oh five by Dave Duffield and Aneel Bhusri, after Oracle took over Duffield's company, PeopleSoft. It was built for the cloud from day one.

Its two core products are Workday Human Capital Management, covering HR, recruiting and payroll, and Workday Financial Management, covering accounting, procurement and expenses.

What's different? One data model for people and money, so headcount and costs line up. Two major updates a year for every customer. A mobile-first design. And strong reporting with a full audit trail.

Workday is especially common in services firms, higher education, healthcare and the public sector, where people are the biggest cost.

So Workday is the people-first cloud ERP, and we'll meet it again in the payroll cycle.

Quick check. What was Workday originally best known for? Pause and pick.

Human capital management: HR and payroll.

Microteach 5.5 · 1:20Mid-market and small-business systems

Small businesses usually start with accounting software rather than a full ERP.

Read the script

Not every company needs SAP. Most US businesses run on something smaller.

Small businesses usually start with accounting software rather than a full ERP. QuickBooks Online leads the US small business market, alongside Xero and FreshBooks.

In the mid-market you'll meet Sage Intacct, a cloud financial system popular with nonprofits and services firms; Acumatica, a flexible cloud ERP; Epicor, strong in manufacturing and distribution; and Infor, whose suites are built for specific industries.

For mid-sized firms, industry fit often matters more than brand. A food distributor needs lot tracking. A construction firm needs job costing. Specialized systems offer these out of the box.

Remember the difference. Accounting software records transactions. ERP integrates every business process around one database. QuickBooks is not an ERP.

So the goal is to right-size the system to the business.

Quick check. Which of these is accounting software rather than a full ERP? Pause and pick.

QuickBooks Online.

Microteach 5.6 · 1:20How companies choose an ERP

The market is often split into tiers.

Read the script

Choosing an ERP can be a ten-year commitment. Here's how companies decide.

The market is often split into tiers. Tier one: SAP, Oracle Fusion and Workday, for large global firms. Tier two: Dynamics 365, NetSuite and Infor, for the mid-market. Tier three: small business tools like QuickBooks. The lines blur, but the idea helps.

The selection process: define your requirements, issue a request for proposal, watch scripted vendor demos, run a fit-gap analysis, calculate total cost of ownership, then negotiate and select.

A fit-gap analysis compares your processes to the software's standard features. Gaps are closed by configuration, customization, or changing your process. Changing the process is often cheapest.

Total cost of ownership goes well beyond the license: the implementation partner, data migration, training, and years of support and upgrades.

So start with your requirements, not the vendor's sales pitch.

Quick check. In a fit-gap analysis, what is a gap? Pause and pick.

A process the software doesn't support as standard.
