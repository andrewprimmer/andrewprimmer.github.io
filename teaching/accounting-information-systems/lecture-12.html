Microteach 12.1 · 1:20General controls versus application controls

IT general controls, or ITGCs, apply to the whole IT environment.

Read the script

IT controls come in two layers: the foundation, and the checks built into each application.

IT general controls, or ITGCs, apply to the whole IT environment. They make sure systems are secure and changes are controlled.

ITGCs cover four areas: access to programs and data, program changes, new system development, and computer operations like backups and job scheduling.

Application controls are built into specific applications. They check individual transactions for accuracy, completeness and validity.

Examples: the three-way match, credit limit checks, edit checks on input, and automatic calculations like tax or depreciation.

Here's the key. If ITGCs are weak, you can't rely on any application control. If anyone can change the program, the three-way match might have been switched off.

So: general controls are the foundation, and application controls are built on top.

Quick check. Controlling who can change programs is which kind of control? Pause and pick.

An IT general control.

Microteach 12.2 · 1:15COBIT 2019

COBIT, from the IT governance association ISACA, is a framework for governing and managing enterprise information and technology.

Read the script

COSO covers internal control overall. For IT governance, the go-to framework is COBIT.

COBIT, from the IT governance association ISACA, is a framework for governing and managing enterprise information and technology. The version most organizations use is COBIT twenty nineteen.

COBIT separates governance, where the board evaluates, directs and monitors, from management, which plans, builds, runs and monitors IT day to day.

Management has four domains: align, plan and organize; build, acquire and implement; deliver, service and support; and monitor, evaluate and assess.

The core principle: IT exists to meet stakeholder needs and create value for the business, not for its own sake.

ISACA also runs the CISA credential: Certified Information Systems Auditor. Many IT auditors hold it alongside the CPA.

So: COSO for internal control, and COBIT for IT governance.

Quick check. Who publishes COBIT? Pause and pick.

ISACA.

Microteach 12.3 · 1:14Access controls

Authentication proves who you are.

Read the script

Most data breaches don't need clever hacking. They need a stolen password.

Authentication proves who you are. There are three types of factor: something you know, like a password; something you have, like a phone or token; and something you are, like a fingerprint.

Multi-factor authentication, or MFA, requires two or more different types of factor. A stolen password alone is then not enough.

After authentication comes authorization: what you're allowed to do. In an ERP, that's set by your roles.

The principle of least privilege says users get only the access they need for their job, and nothing more.

Access needs ongoing care: remove leavers promptly, have managers review access regularly, watch privileged users closely, and log and monitor activity.

So: authentication asks who you are. Authorization asks what you can do.

Quick check. A password plus a code sent to your phone is an example of what? Pause and pick.

Multi-factor authentication.

Microteach 12.4 · 1:17Encryption and confidentiality

Encryption transforms readable data, called plaintext, into unreadable ciphertext.

Read the script

If a laptop full of customer data is stolen, encryption is what keeps that data safe.

Encryption transforms readable data, called plaintext, into unreadable ciphertext. Only someone with the right key can turn it back.

Symmetric encryption uses one shared key to encrypt and decrypt. It's fast. Asymmetric encryption uses a pair: a public key to encrypt, and a private key to decrypt.

Protect data in transit, as it moves across networks, using HTTPS or a VPN. And protect it at rest, on drives and in databases.

Asymmetric keys also create digital signatures, proving who sent a document and that it hasn't been changed. That's how electronic contracts and payments are verified.

Confidentiality also means classifying sensitive data, encrypting it, restricting access, and disposing of it securely when it's no longer needed.

With good encryption, lost data is still safe data.

Quick check. What does asymmetric encryption use? Pause and pick.

A public key and a private key.

Microteach 12.5 · 1:21Input, processing and output controls

Input controls catch errors at entry.

Read the script

Processing integrity means the data that goes in, comes out right.

Input controls catch errors at entry. A field check makes sure a numeric field holds numbers. A limit check rejects, say, over sixty hours a week. A validity check confirms a customer number exists. A completeness check makes sure required fields are filled. And batch totals confirm a whole batch was entered.

Processing controls make sure data is processed correctly: matching records, file labels, recalculating totals, and preventing two users from updating the same record at once.

Output controls protect the results: users review reports, outputs are reconciled to inputs, sensitive reports go only to authorized people, and printouts are securely destroyed.

A clever example is the hash total: adding up something meaningless, like all the customer numbers in a batch. If the total changes, something was lost or altered.

So: control the data going in, during processing, and coming out.

Quick check. Rejecting a time card showing ninety hours in one week is which kind of check? Pause and pick.

A limit check.

Microteach 12.6 · 1:14Availability and disaster recovery

Availability means systems are running when the business needs them.

Read the script

What happens to the business if the ERP goes down for a week?

Availability means systems are running when the business needs them. Threats include hardware failure, power outages, natural disasters and ransomware.

Two measures guide planning. The recovery point objective, or RPO, is how much data you can afford to lose. The recovery time objective, or RTO, is how quickly the system must be back.

Backups come in full and incremental forms. Keep copies off-site and offline, so ransomware can't reach them, and test restoring them regularly.

A disaster recovery plan restores IT. A business continuity plan keeps the whole business running. Recovery sites range from hot, ready immediately, to cold, an empty facility.

Remember: an untested plan is not a plan. Test backups, and run recovery drills.

Quick check. The amount of data a company can afford to lose is measured by what? Pause and pick.

The recovery point objective, or RPO.
