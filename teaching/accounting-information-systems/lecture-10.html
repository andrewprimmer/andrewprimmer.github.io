Microteach 10.1 · 1:02Record to report in 2 minutes

The revenue, expenditure, production and payroll cycles all post their entries to the general ledger.

Read the script

Every cycle we've studied ends in one place: the general ledger.

The revenue, expenditure, production and payroll cycles all post their entries to the general ledger.

The record to report process: update the ledger from the subledgers, post adjusting entries, prepare the financial statements, and produce management reports.

The detail lives in subledgers, like accounts receivable by customer. The general ledger holds the control account totals. The two must reconcile.

Outputs include the financial statements, tax returns, SEC filings for public companies, and management dashboards.

So everything flows into the ledger, and everything reported flows out of it.

Quick check. Where is the detail of accounts receivable by customer kept? Pause and pick.

In a subledger, which reconciles to the GL control account.

Microteach 10.2 · 1:15Designing a chart of accounts

The chart of accounts is the list and coding structure of every general ledger account.

Read the script

Want a report your ERP can't produce? The problem is probably the chart of accounts.

The chart of accounts is the list and coding structure of every general ledger account. Every transaction is posted using it.

A typical numbering scheme puts assets in the one thousands, liabilities in the two thousands, equity in the three thousands, revenue in the four thousands, and expenses from five thousand up.

Modern ERPs use segments. One code combines the natural account with department, location and product, so you can slice results many ways.

For example, one code can mean product sales, in department twenty, in New York. Now you can report sales by department, or by region.

Design tips: plan for the reports you'll need, leave gaps in the numbering so you can grow, keep it as simple as possible, and control who can add accounts.

Remember: you can only report what you code.

Quick check. In a typical chart of accounts, revenue accounts start with which number? Pause and pick.

Four.

Microteach 10.3 · 1:18Automated and manual journal entries

Most journal entries are automated.

Read the script

Auditors don't worry much about millions of automatic entries. They worry about the manual ones.

Most journal entries are automated. When a subledger records a sale, a receipt or a payroll run, the ERP posts the entry automatically, using configured rules.

Manual journal entries are typed in by people: accruals, corrections, reclassifications and adjustments.

Manual entries are riskier because they bypass the subledger controls. Many financial statement frauds used manual entries to adjust the books, especially at period end.

Controls: require support for every manual entry, approval before posting, restricted posting access, and review of unusual entries.

Auditors test for red flags: round amounts, entries posted on weekends or holidays, entries made by senior managers, entries right before period end, and unusual account combinations.

So: automate the routine, and scrutinize the manual.

Quick check. Which journal entries carry more fraud risk? Pause and pick.

Manual journal entries.

Microteach 10.4 · 1:08The month-end close

The month-end close: cut off the period's transactions, reconcile key accounts, post accruals and adjustments, review the trial balance, and prepare the financial…

Read the script

Closing the books used to take weeks. The best companies now do it in days.

The month-end close: cut off the period's transactions, reconcile key accounts, post accruals and adjustments, review the trial balance, and prepare the financial statements.

Key reconciliations include the bank reconciliation, subledgers to the general ledger, intercompany accounts, and inventory to the physical count.

Teams use a close checklist: every task, who owns it, and the day it's due. Many use dedicated tools like BlackLine or FloQast, linked to the ERP.

The trend is continuous accounting: reconciling and reviewing throughout the month, so the close itself is fast and mostly automated.

So: reconcile, adjust, review and report, with as much automation as possible.

Quick check. Comparing the bank statement to the ledger cash balance is called what? Pause and pick.

A bank reconciliation.

Microteach 10.5 · 1:13XBRL and SEC reporting

US public companies file a 10-K each year, and a 10-Q each quarter, with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Read the script

When a public company files its annual report, computers read it before people do.

US public companies file a 10-K each year, and a 10-Q each quarter, with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

XBRL, or extensible business reporting language, tags each number in a filing so software can read it. Revenue is tagged as revenue, not just a number on a page.

Today the SEC requires inline XBRL: the tags are embedded in the human-readable document itself.

The tags are used by investors and analysts, the SEC's own reviewers, financial data providers, and researchers comparing thousands of companies at once.

Tagging is part of the reporting process, so it needs controls. A wrong tag means wrong data flowing to every user.

So XBRL turns financial statements into data that computers can read.

Quick check. What does XBRL do? Pause and pick.

It tags financial data so software can read it.

Microteach 10.6 · 1:07Dashboards and the balanced scorecard

A dashboard shows managers key measures at a glance, often updated in real time from the ERP or data warehouse, using tools like Power BI or Tableau.

Read the script

Financial statements tell you what happened last quarter. Dashboards tell you what's happening now.

A dashboard shows managers key measures at a glance, often updated in real time from the ERP or data warehouse, using tools like Power BI or Tableau.

The balanced scorecard, from Kaplan and Norton, balances four perspectives: financial, customer, internal business process, and learning and growth, all linked to strategy.

Good key performance indicators are linked to strategy, measurable and timely, few rather than hundreds, and each one is owned by someone.

Mix leading and lagging indicators. Profit is lagging: it reports the past. Customer satisfaction is leading: it predicts future sales.

So: measure what matters, across more than just the financials.

Quick check. Which of these is a leading indicator? Pause and pick.

Customer satisfaction. It predicts future sales.
