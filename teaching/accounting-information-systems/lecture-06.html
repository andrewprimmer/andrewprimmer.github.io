Microteach 6.1 · 0:59Order to cash in 2 minutes

The revenue cycle, or order to cash, has four basic activities: sales order entry, shipping, billing, and cash collections.

Read the script

Every dollar of revenue travels the same four-step path.

The revenue cycle, or order to cash, has four basic activities: sales order entry, shipping, billing, and cash collections.

Each step creates documents: the sales order, the picking list for the warehouse, the packing slip and bill of lading for the shipment, the sales invoice, and the customer's remittance advice with their payment.

Every step feeds the general ledger: sales revenue, accounts receivable, cash, inventory and cost of goods sold.

The goals: ship the right goods on time, bill accurately, collect every dollar owed, and protect customer data.

So remember: order, ship, bill, collect. We'll take each step in turn.

Sales order entry.

Microteach 6.2 · 1:14Sales orders and credit approval

Sales order entry has four steps: take the customer's order, check their credit, check that inventory is available, and confirm the order to the customer.

Read the script

The cheapest bad debt is the one you never let happen.

Sales order entry has four steps: take the customer's order, check their credit, check that inventory is available, and confirm the order to the customer.

The key control is the credit limit: the maximum balance a customer is allowed to owe. The ERP checks every new order against it automatically.

Example. A customer owes fifteen thousand dollars and has a twenty thousand dollar limit. A new eight thousand dollar order would take them over, so the ERP holds it for a credit manager to approve.

Credit approval must be separate from sales. Salespeople are paid to sell, so they shouldn't decide who gets credit.

Other controls: edit checks during data entry, accurate customer master data, authentication for online orders, and an available-to-promise check on inventory.

So: approve credit before you ship, and keep that decision away from sales.

Quick check. Who should approve credit for a new order? Pause and pick.

The credit department, separate from sales.

Microteach 6.3 · 1:11Picking, packing and shipping

Shipping starts when the ERP issues a picking list.

Read the script

The warehouse is where the revenue cycle meets the physical world.

Shipping starts when the ERP issues a picking list. Warehouse staff pick the goods, pack them, check the count, and ship them with a bill of lading.

Three documents matter. The picking list tells staff what to pull from the shelves. The packing slip lists what's in the box. And the bill of lading is the legal contract with the carrier.

The key control is comparison. The quantity picked should match the order, and the quantity shipped should match the packing slip. Barcode scanning makes this automatic.

Threats here include shipping the wrong items, sending goods to the wrong address, theft of inventory, and shipping without an approved order.

In an ERP, recording the shipment triggers the invoice. A shipment that's never recorded is revenue that's never billed.

Pick, pack, check, ship, and record it, because billing depends on it.

Quick check. Which document is the legal contract with the carrier? Pause and pick.

The bill of lading.

Microteach 6.4 · 1:24Billing and revenue recognition

When the shipment is recorded, the ERP matches it to the sales order, creates the invoice using prices from master data, and posts the account receivable.

Read the script

Shipping the goods isn't the end. You still have to bill, and recognize revenue correctly.

When the shipment is recorded, the ERP matches it to the sales order, creates the invoice using prices from master data, and posts the account receivable.

Revenue recognition in the US follows ASC six oh six, issued by the Financial Accounting Standards Board.

It has five steps: identify the contract, identify the performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate the price to the obligations, and recognize revenue as each obligation is satisfied.

For a simple sale, revenue is recognized at shipment. But for bundles, subscriptions or multi-year deals, the ERP's revenue module allocates and schedules revenue over time.

Billing controls: bill only for recorded shipments, take prices from approved master data, number invoices in sequence, and reconcile shipments to invoices so nothing is missed.

So: ship, then bill, then recognize revenue under the five steps.

Quick check. Under ASC six oh six, when is revenue recognized? Pause and pick.

When each performance obligation is satisfied.

Microteach 6.5 · 1:24Cash collections and lockboxes

Cash collection is the last step: receiving customer payments, depositing them, and applying them to the right invoices.

Read the script

The person who opens the mail should never be the person who records the payment.

Cash collection is the last step: receiving customer payments, depositing them, and applying them to the right invoices.

The key control is segregation of duties. Whoever handles the cash must not also record it in the receivables records, or they could steal a payment and hide it.

Without that separation, you risk lapping. An employee steals customer A's payment, covers it with customer B's payment, then covers B with C's, and so on.

Better methods reduce the risk. With a lockbox, customers mail payments straight to the bank, which deposits them and sends the remittance data. Many firms also use electronic lockboxes, ACH transfers, wires and online card payments.

The remittance advice tells you which invoice a payment covers, so cash is applied correctly. Modern ERPs use AI to match payments to invoices automatically.

So: separate cash handling from record keeping, and get the cash into the bank fast.

Quick check. Covering one customer's stolen payment with a later customer's payment is called what? Pause and pick.

Lapping.

Microteach 6.6 · 1:23Revenue cycle threats and controls

Match threat to control.

Read the script

Here's the revenue cycle's most-wanted list of threats, and the controls that stop them.

Match threat to control. Sales to bad credit customers: credit limits. Fictitious sales: validate orders against real customers. Shipping errors: barcode checks. Billing errors: prices from approved master data. Theft of cash: segregation of duties and lockboxes.

Fictitious revenue is the big financial reporting risk. Managers under pressure may record fake sales or ship early to hit targets, which is why auditors focus on sales near year end.

Some controls protect the whole cycle: restricted system access, reconciling the receivables subledger to the general ledger, reviewing exception reports, and sending customers monthly statements.

That last one is clever. Customers become your auditors. If a payment they made isn't on their statement, they'll call.

So for every step in order to cash, ask: what could go wrong, and what control prevents or detects it?

Quick check. Monthly customer statements mainly help detect what? Pause and pick.

Lapping and billing errors, because customers spot payments that weren't credited.
