ACCT 312 Intermediate Accounting II

Intangible assets, liabilities, stockholders' equity, earnings per share, investments, revenue recognition, accounting changes and the statement of cash flows, in 14 lectures and 84 two-minute videos, with a slide deck and an interactive exercise set for every lecture.

Required textbook

Intermediate Accounting, 19th edition

Donald E. Kieso, Jerry J. Weygandt, Terry D. Warfield and Laura D. Wiley. Wiley, 2025. WileyPLUS optional.

The standard US intermediate accounting text, closely aligned with the CPA exam. ACCT 312 and ACCT 416 share this one book and split its chapters between them with no overlap: if you took ACCT 416, you already own it.

ACCT 312 reads Chapters 12–18, 22 and 23. ACCT 416 Intermediate Accounting I reads Chapters 1–11. Chapters 19–21 and 24 (income taxes, pensions, leases, full disclosure) aren't part of either course.

A continuation of ACCT 416 Intermediate Accounting I (ACCT 311 at some institutions). Topics: intangible assets, current liabilities, long-term liabilities, stockholders' equity, earnings per share, revenue recognition, investments, accounting changes and the statement of cash flows. Prerequisite: C- or better in ECON 201, ECON 202 and ACCT 311 or ACCT 416. The running case continues with Bluestem Outdoor of Marshall, Minnesota as it buys a competitor, issues bonds, goes public, rewards employees with stock and builds a long-term contract business, with CFO Maria Okafor and controller Sam Nygaard.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook chapter for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set (112 questions in all), then the textbook's end-of-chapter problems.
Every lecture has a textbook chapter, six videos, a two-hour slide deck and an interactive exercise set. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Lectures

Part one · Intangibles and liabilities

  1. L1Intangible assets and goodwillCharacteristics and recognition of intangible assets, amortization of limited-life intangibles, types of intangibles, research and development costs, goodwill in a business combination, and impairment of intangibles and goodwill. Read: Chapter 12.Week 1
  2. L2Current liabilities, provisions and contingenciesAccounts and notes payable, current maturities and refinancing, unearned revenue and gift cards, sales taxes and payroll, loss and gain contingencies, assurance warranties, and asset retirement obligations. Read: Chapter 13.Week 2
  3. L3Bonds payableTypes of bonds, pricing bonds at the market rate, issuing at par and between interest dates, the effective-interest method for discounts and premiums, debt issuance costs, and year-end accruals. Read: Chapter 14.Week 3
  4. L4Long-term notes, extinguishment and debt reportingNotes payable at present value and imputed interest, extinguishment of debt before maturity, the fair value option, troubled debt settlements, off-balance-sheet financing, and presentation and analysis of long-term debt. Read: Chapter 14.Week 4

Part two · Stockholders' equity

  1. L5Stockholders' equity: issuing and reacquiring sharesThe corporate form and components of equity, par value and share counts, issuing stock for cash and noncash consideration, issuance costs, treasury stock under the cost method, retiring shares, and presentation and ratios. Read: Chapter 15.Week 5
  2. L6Preferred stock, dividends and stock splitsFeatures of preferred stock, dividend dates and cash dividends, cumulative dividend preferences, property and liquidating dividends, stock dividends and stock splits, and retained earnings restrictions and payout. Read: Chapter 15.Week 6
  3. L7Dilutive securities and stock-based compensationConvertible bonds and preferred stock, induced conversions, stock warrants issued with bonds, stock options measured at fair value over the service period, restricted stock, employee stock purchase plans and stock appreciation rights. Read: Chapter 16.Week 7
  4. L8Earnings per share: basic and dilutedSimple and complex capital structures, weighted-average shares with issuances, buybacks, stock dividends and splits, basic EPS with preferred dividends, diluted EPS using the if-converted and treasury stock methods, antidilution and presentation. Read: Chapter 16.Week 9

Part three · Investments and revenue

  1. L9Investments in debt securitiesWhy companies invest, classifying debt securities as held-to-maturity, available-for-sale or trading, amortized cost and the effective-interest method, fair value adjustments through OCI or net income, sales and reclassification, credit losses and transfers. Read: Chapter 17.Week 10
  2. L10Investments in equity securitiesLevels of influence, equity securities at fair value through net income, the measurement alternative, the equity method, excess of cost over book value, consolidation and control, and presentation. Read: Chapter 17.Week 11
  3. L11Revenue recognition: the five-step modelThe core principle of Topic 606, identifying contracts and performance obligations, determining the transaction price including variable consideration, allocating by standalone selling prices, recognizing revenue at a point in time or over time, and contract assets, liabilities and costs. Read: Chapter 18.Week 12
  4. L12Revenue recognition: special issues and long-term contractsSales with a right of return, service-type warranties, principal versus agent, consignments and bill-and-hold arrangements, revenue over time on long-term contracts using cost-to-cost, and losses on contracts. Read: Chapter 18.Week 13

Part four · Changes and cash flows

  1. L13Accounting changes and error analysisTypes of accounting changes, retrospective application of changes in principle, impracticability and indirect effects, prospective changes in estimate including depreciation methods, corrections of errors as prior period adjustments, and counterbalancing and noncounterbalancing errors. Read: Chapter 22.Week 14
  2. L14The statement of cash flowsPurpose and classification of cash flows, the direct and indirect methods, adjustments for noncash items, gains and losses, and working capital, investing and financing activities, noncash transactions, and a recap of the course. Read: Chapter 23.Week 15
