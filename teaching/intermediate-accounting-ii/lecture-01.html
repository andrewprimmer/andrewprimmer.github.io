Microteach 1.1 · 1:20What intangible assets are

Purchased intangibles are capitalized; internally created ones are mostly expensed; limited lives are amortized.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Welcome to Intermediate Accounting Two. Bluestem Outdoor is growing up: new brands, new debt, and soon, public shareholders. We start with assets you can't touch.

Intangible assets lack physical substance and aren't financial instruments. Patents, trademarks, customer lists, licenses and goodwill are examples. Their value comes from rights and advantages.

Purchased intangibles are recorded at cost, including legal fees and other costs to acquire them. Internally created ones are mostly expensed as incurred: only direct costs like legal and registration fees are capitalized.

Intangibles with a limited life are amortized over their useful life, usually straight-line, and tested for impairment when events suggest it. Those with an indefinite life, like a renewable trademark, aren't amortized but are tested at least annually.

Use the shorter of legal life and economic life. A patent may last twenty years legally but be obsolete in six.

Quick check. Which intangible is not amortized? Pause and pick.

It's C, the indefinitely renewable trademark. It's tested for impairment instead.

Microteach 1.2 · 1:16Types of intangibles: the patent

Six categories of intangibles; a patent's cost and successful defense costs are amortized over its useful life.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Bluestem buys a patent on a folding-kayak hinge from an inventor in Duluth.

Intangibles fall into six groups: marketing-related, like trademarks; customer-related, like customer lists; artistic-related, like copyrights; contract-related, like franchises and licenses; technology-related, like patents; and goodwill.

Bluestem pays $180,000 including legal fees. The patent has fifteen years of legal life left, but Bluestem expects the design to stay useful for six. Amortization is $30,000 a year.

Amortization is often credited directly to the asset account, though an accumulated amortization account is also used.

If Bluestem successfully defends the patent in court, the $24,000 of legal costs are added to the patent and amortized over its remaining life. If it loses, the costs are expensed and the patent written off.

Quick check. Patent cost ninety thousand; legal life twelve years; useful life five. Annual amortization? Pause and pick.

It's B, $18,000.

Microteach 1.3 · 1:23Research and development costs

US GAAP expenses R&D as incurred; IFRS capitalizes development costs that meet criteria.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Bluestem spends $350,000 designing an electric-assist kayak. Is that an asset?

Under US GAAP, research and development costs are expensed as incurred, because their future benefits are too uncertain. That includes the salaries, materials, prototypes and testing.

Engineers' salaries of $210,000, prototypes of $80,000, outside consultants of $45,000, and depreciation of $15,000 on a test tank are all R&D expense: $350,000.

Notice the test tank. Because it has alternative future uses, Bluestem capitalized it and charges only the year's depreciation to R&D. Equipment built for one project only is expensed immediately.

IFRS expenses research but capitalizes development costs once technical feasibility and other criteria are met. US GAAP has similar rules only for software. Not R&D at all: routine product improvements and quality control.

Quick check. Under US GAAP, prototype testing costs for a new kayak are what? Pause and pick.

It's B, expensed as R&D.

Microteach 1.4 · 1:22Goodwill

Goodwill = purchase price − fair value of identifiable net assets; it's recorded only in a business combination.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Bluestem buys Northwind Canoe Company, a respected canoe maker, for $2.4 million cash.

Bluestem identifies and values every asset it acquires at fair value, including the Northwind trademark and customer list that weren't on Northwind's books. Identifiable assets total $2.6 million, less $700,000 of liabilities: $1.9 million. It paid $2.4 million, so goodwill is $500,000.

Goodwill is the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of identifiable net assets: reputation, workforce, synergies. It's recorded only when a business is acquired. Internally generated goodwill is never recorded.

Public companies don't amortize goodwill; they test it for impairment. Private companies like Bluestem today can elect to amortize it over ten years or less.

If the price were less than the fair value of net assets, after rechecking the values, Bluestem would record a gain on bargain purchase.

Quick check. Price five million; fair value of identifiable assets six million; liabilities two million. Goodwill? Pause and pick.

It's A, $1 million.

Microteach 1.5 · 1:22Impairment of intangibles and goodwill

Limited-life intangibles use the recoverability test; indefinite-life and goodwill compare carrying amount with fair value.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Two years after the purchase, canoe sales slump. Are the Northwind intangibles impaired?

Limited-life intangibles use the same two-step test as PP&E. Indefinite-life intangibles skip recoverability: if carrying amount exceeds fair value, the excess is the loss. Goodwill is tested at the reporting unit level.

The Northwind reporting unit's carrying amount, including $500,000 of goodwill, is $2.35 million. Its fair value is $2.1 million. The $250,000 excess is the goodwill impairment loss, limited to the goodwill balance.

Bluestem records a $250,000 loss in continuing operations. Goodwill impairments can never be reversed.

Companies may first do a qualitative assessment: if it's not more likely than not that the unit's fair value is below carrying amount, no further test is needed that year.

Quick check. Reporting unit carrying amount eight million, including one million of goodwill; fair value six point five million. Goodwill impairment? Pause and pick.

It's B, $1 million: limited to the goodwill balance.

Microteach 1.6 · 1:11Other costs and presentation

Start-up, organization, advertising and initial operating losses are expensed; intangibles are shown separately.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 12.

Read the script

Bluestem opens a new store in Minneapolis. Can the opening costs be an asset?

No. Start-up and pre-opening costs, organization costs, advertising and initial operating losses are expensed as incurred, however much future benefit management expects.

Training of $18,000 and $25,000 of opening advertising are expenses. Only the $140,000 of store fixtures, which are property, plant and equipment, are capitalized.

On the balance sheet, goodwill appears on its own line, and other intangibles net of amortization. Amortization and impairment losses go in continuing operations. Notes show classes, useful lives and the next five years' expected amortization.

The irony: Bluestem's own brand, perhaps its most valuable asset, isn't on its balance sheet. Northwind's brand is, because Bluestem bought it.

Quick check. Which of these is capitalized? Pause and pick.

It's C, the purchased customer list.
