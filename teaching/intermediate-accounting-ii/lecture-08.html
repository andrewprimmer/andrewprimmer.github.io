Microteach 8.1 · 1:21Why EPS, and which EPS

Public companies report basic EPS, and diluted EPS when they have potentially dilutive securities.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

Bluestem now has public shareholders, convertible bonds and employee options. Its EPS just got complicated.

Earnings per share is income available to common shareholders divided by the weighted-average number of common shares outstanding. Public companies must report it on the face of the income statement.

A simple capital structure has only common and nonconvertible preferred stock: report basic EPS. A complex structure has securities that could become common shares, like convertibles, options and warrants: report basic and diluted EPS.

Diluted EPS shows what EPS would be if every dilutive security had been converted or exercised. It's a worst-case view for current shareholders.

Bluestem's 2030 net income is $1.2 million. It has $500,000 of six percent cumulative preferred, $1 million of convertible bonds, and 30,000 employee options. We'll build both EPS figures.

Quick check. A company with only common stock and nonconvertible preferred reports what? Pause and pick.

It's B, basic EPS only.

Microteach 8.2 · 1:18Weighted-average shares

Weight shares by time outstanding; restate retroactively for stock dividends and splits.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

The share count changed during 2030. EPS uses a weighted average.

Bluestem began the year with 500,000 shares. It issued 60,000 on April 1, outstanding for nine months: 45,000 weighted shares. It bought back 20,000 on October 1, out for three months: minus 5,000. The weighted average is 540,000.

Stock dividends and splits are different. They don't bring in resources, so they're treated as if they'd happened at the beginning of the year, and at the start of every prior period presented.

If Bluestem had split its shares two for one on November 1, every share outstanding before the split would be doubled, back to January 1. The weighted average would be 1,080,000, and prior years' EPS would be restated too.

Even a split after year-end but before the statements are issued is applied retroactively, so readers see EPS on the new share basis.

Quick check. January 1, a hundred thousand shares; July 1, forty thousand issued. Weighted average? Pause and pick.

It's B, 120,000.

Microteach 8.3 · 1:22Basic EPS

Subtract the current year's preferred dividend: declared, or cumulative even if not declared.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

Now the numerator. Common shareholders only get what's left after preferred.

Subtract the current year's preferred dividend. For cumulative preferred, subtract it whether or not it's declared. For noncumulative, only if declared. Arrears from earlier years were already counted in those years.

Bluestem subtracts $30,000 of preferred dividends from $1.2 million, leaving $1,170,000 for common. Divided by 540,000 weighted shares, basic EPS is $2.17.

If Bluestem had discontinued operations, it would report per-share amounts for income from continuing operations, the discontinued operations, and net income, as you saw in Intermediate One.

The most common error: dividing net income, not income available to common, by the shares.

Quick check. Net income eight hundred thousand; five percent cumulative preferred, one million par, no dividends declared; three hundred thousand weighted shares. Basic EPS? Pause and pick.

It's B, $2.50. Cumulative dividends are deducted even if not declared.

Microteach 8.4 · 1:20Diluted EPS: convertible securities

If-converted method: add back after-tax interest and add the shares as if converted at the start of the year.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

What if all Bluestem's convertible bondholders had converted on January 1?

The if-converted method assumes convertible securities were converted at the beginning of the year, or when issued if later. Two things change: the interest wouldn't have been paid, and there'd be more shares.

Interest of $40,000 would be saved, but taxes would rise by $10,000, so income rises by $30,000. Shares rise by 50,000. The per-share effect is sixty cents, below basic EPS, so the bonds are dilutive.

For convertible preferred stock, assume conversion: don't subtract its dividends from the numerator, and add the common shares to the denominator. No tax effect, since dividends aren't deductible.

A convertible is included only if it lowers EPS. If its per-share effect is above EPS, it's antidilutive and left out.

Quick check. Convertible bonds: interest sixty thousand; tax twenty-five percent; convertible into thirty thousand shares. Per-share effect? Pause and pick.

It's B, $1.50: forty-five thousand over thirty thousand.

Microteach 8.5 · 1:31Diluted EPS: options and warrants

Treasury stock method: assume exercise, and assume proceeds buy back shares at the average price.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

Bluestem's designers hold 30,000 options at $16. The share price averaged $24 this year.

The treasury stock method assumes options are exercised and the cash received is used to buy back shares at the average market price. Only the net new shares are added.

Exercising 30,000 options brings in $480,000. At an average price of $24, that buys back 20,000 shares. The net increase is 10,000 shares, with no change to income.

Options and warrants are dilutive only when they're in the money, with an exercise price below the average market price. Otherwise the method would reduce shares, which is antidilutive.

Putting it together: income of $1,170,000 plus the $30,000 of after-tax interest gives $1.2 million. Shares are 540,000 plus 10,000 from the options and 50,000 from the bonds: 600,000. Diluted EPS is $2.00, against basic EPS of $2.17.

Quick check. Ten thousand warrants at thirty dollars; average market price forty. Incremental shares? Pause and pick.

It's B, 2,500: 300,000 buys back 7,500.

Microteach 8.6 · 1:19Antidilution and presentation

Exclude antidilutive securities; include securities from most to least dilutive; present basic and diluted EPS.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 16.

Read the script

Diluted EPS should never be higher than basic. Antidilution rules make sure of it.

A security is antidilutive if including it would increase EPS or reduce loss per share. Antidilutive securities are excluded from diluted EPS.

With several securities, rank them by per-share effect. Options and warrants, with no income effect, go first. Then convertibles from the lowest effect upward. Stop when the next one would raise EPS.

If a company has a loss from continuing operations, any additional shares would reduce the loss per share, so all potential shares are antidilutive and diluted EPS equals basic.

Bluestem reports basic EPS of $2.17 and diluted EPS of $2.00 on the face of the income statement, with a note reconciling numerators and denominators, and listing securities excluded as antidilutive.

Quick check. Basic EPS one eighty. A convertible's per-share effect is two ten. Include it in diluted EPS? Pause and pick.

It's B, no. It would raise EPS.
