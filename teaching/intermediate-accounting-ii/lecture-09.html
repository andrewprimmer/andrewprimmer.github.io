Microteach 9.1 · 1:19Classifying debt investments

Debt securities are held-to-maturity, available-for-sale or trading, depending on intent and ability.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

After the IPO, Bluestem has more cash than it needs for the winter. It invests some in bonds.

Companies invest to earn a return on idle cash, to line up cash for future needs like a plant expansion, or strategically, to influence or control another company. Strategic stakes are equity investments, next lecture.

Debt securities fall into three categories. Held-to-maturity securities are carried at amortized cost. Available-for-sale securities are carried at fair value, with unrealized gains and losses in OCI. Trading securities are at fair value, with gains and losses in net income.

Held-to-maturity requires both the positive intent and the ability to hold the bonds until they mature. Selling them early can taint the whole category.

Trading securities are bought and held mainly to sell in the near term, as a bank's trading desk does. Available-for-sale is the residual: neither held-to-maturity nor trading.

Quick check. Unrealized gains on available-for-sale debt securities are reported where? Pause and pick.

It's B, other comprehensive income.

Microteach 9.2 · 1:38Held-to-maturity: amortized cost

Record at cost; interest revenue = carrying amount × market rate; fair value changes are ignored.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

On January 1, 2030, Bluestem buys $500,000 of five-year, six percent bonds issued by a utility, paying interest semiannually. Similar bonds yield eight percent.

Ten periods at four percent: the principal is worth $337,780 and the interest $121,664. Bluestem pays $459,444, a discount from face.

It records Debt Investments at cost, $459,444. Bluestem intends and is able to hold the bonds to maturity.

On June 30, interest revenue is four percent of the carrying amount, $18,378. Cash received is $15,000. The $3,378 difference increases the investment to $462,822. It's the mirror of what an issuer does.

For held-to-maturity securities, changes in fair value aren't recognized, because Bluestem will collect the contractual cash flows. Fair value is disclosed in the notes.

Quick check. A held-to-maturity bond's carrying amount is ninety-six thousand; market rate five percent per period; cash interest four thousand. Interest revenue? Pause and pick.

It's B, $4,800.

Microteach 9.3 · 1:38Available-for-sale: fair value through OCI

Amortize as usual, then adjust to fair value at year-end with the change in OCI.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

Suppose Bluestem might sell the utility bonds if it needs cash for the second plant. Then they're available-for-sale.

Interest revenue and amortization are recorded exactly as before. After two periods, amortized cost is $466,335. At December 31, the bonds' fair value is $470,000: an unrealized holding gain of $3,665.

Bluestem debits a Fair Value Adjustment account, which sits alongside the investment at amortized cost, and credits Unrealized Holding Gain or Loss, Equity: other comprehensive income.

The balance sheet shows the investment at fair value, $470,000. Net income includes interest revenue of $36,891 for the year. The $3,665 gain goes to other comprehensive income and accumulates in equity.

Why OCI? Bluestem isn't trying to profit from short-term price changes, so putting the swings in net income could mislead. OCI shows them without distorting operating results.

Quick check. Available-for-sale debt securities: amortized cost two ten thousand; fair value two oh three thousand. The adjustment? Pause and pick.

It's B, a $7,000 unrealized loss in OCI, unless it's a credit loss.

Microteach 9.4 · 1:15Trading securities

Trading securities are at fair value with unrealized gains and losses in net income.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

Maria Okafor sets up a small treasury account that actively trades short-term corporate bonds.

The portfolio cost $200,000 and is worth $194,000 at year-end: an unrealized holding loss of $6,000.

For trading securities, the loss goes straight into net income. Trading securities are usually current assets, and interest revenue is recorded as earned; amortization of small discounts is often skipped.

The same price drop has three different results. Held-to-maturity: no change. Available-for-sale: fair value on the balance sheet, loss in OCI. Trading: fair value and a loss in net income.

Because classification changes income, auditors test management's intent against its actual behavior. A company that regularly sells HTM bonds loses the right to use the category.

Quick check. Trading securities: cost eighty thousand; fair value eighty-six thousand. Effect on net income? Pause and pick.

It's B, a $6,000 gain.

Microteach 9.5 · 1:36Selling AFS securities and credit losses

On sale, reclassify accumulated OCI into net income; credit losses go to income through an allowance.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

In 2031, Bluestem sells the utility bonds to fund the second plant.

It sells the bonds for $471,000. The realized gain is measured against amortized cost: $4,665, reported in net income.

The $3,665 gain already in OCI must be removed, or it would be counted twice in comprehensive income: once in OCI last year, and again in this year's realized gain. That's a reclassification adjustment, or recycling.

Credit losses are different. For held-to-maturity bonds, Bluestem records an allowance for expected credit losses under CECL. For available-for-sale bonds, credit losses go to income through an allowance, limited to the amount fair value is below amortized cost. Non-credit declines stay in OCI.

The key distinction: if prices fell because interest rates rose, that's OCI for AFS. If they fell because the issuer may not pay, that's a credit loss in income.

Quick check. Available-for-sale bonds with amortized cost one hundred thousand and a two thousand dollar unrealized gain in AOCI are sold for one oh three thousand. Realized gain in net income? Pause and pick.

It's B, $3,000. The $2,000 in AOCI is reclassified.

Microteach 9.6 · 1:16Transfers and disclosures

Transfers between categories are at fair value; disclose fair values, maturities and gains and losses.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 17.

Read the script

What if Bluestem changes its mind about a bond's category?

Transfers are made at fair value. Moving to trading recognizes any unrealized gain or loss in income right away. Moving from held-to-maturity to available-for-sale puts the unrealized amount in OCI. Moving available-for-sale to held-to-maturity amortizes the OCI amount over the remaining life.

Transfers should be rare, especially into or out of trading, because intent is supposed to be stable.

Notes disclose amortized cost and fair value by type of security, maturities, gross unrealized gains and losses, sales proceeds and realized gains, and the fair value hierarchy levels used.

Companies may also elect the fair value option for any debt security, reporting all changes in net income, much like trading.

Quick check. An available-for-sale bond with four thousand of unrealized gain in AOCI is transferred to trading. What happens to the four thousand? Pause and pick.

It's B, it's recognized in net income now.
