AI and Automation in Accounting

A three-hour practical session. You use AI to write two accounting tools, a ratio analyser and an aged debtor review, and then prove that they are right. You write the spec, the AI writes the code, and you own the numbers.

No coding experience is needed. The AI writes Python, you run it in Google Colab in your browser, and your accounting knowledge does the rest: deciding what the tool should calculate, and catching it when it is wrong. Both datasets have errors planted in them on purpose.

Every company, customer and figure here is fictional. Never paste real client or employer data into a free AI tool.

Before you startSetup and the prompt frame

  1. Sign in to a Google account and open Google Colab. Click New notebook.
  2. Open Gemini or Claude in a second tab.
  3. Download both CSV files above.
  4. Work in pairs. One of you prompts, the other checks. Swap halfway through each practical.

To run code in Colab, paste the AI’s code into a cell and press Shift + Enter. To load a CSV, click the folder icon on the left and upload the file, or run from google.colab import files; files.upload().

Four parts in every prompt

The AI can only be as precise as you are. Every prompt today should have all four parts.

1. ContextWho you are and what the data is.“I have a CSV with columns…”
2. TaskThe calculation, with every formula written out.“Quick ratio = (CA − inventory) / CL”
3. FormatWhat the output should look like.“A table, 2 decimal places”
4. ChecksWhat to flag rather than guess.“Warn if data is missing or negative”

Two habits help: ask the AI to explain each step in a comment, and to print totals before and after each step so you can follow what it did.

Practical oneBuild a ratio analyser

ratios_data.csv · 50 minutes

You will prompt an AI to write a Python tool that calculates five ratios for three companies over three years, then prove it is right.

Step 1. Hand calculation (10 minutes)

Before touching the AI, calculate these for Brindley Engineering Ltd, 2023: revenue 12,400; cost of sales 8,680; operating profit 1,240; current assets 4,960; inventory 2,170; current liabilities 3,100; total debt 2,800; equity 5,600 (all £000). This is your control.

RatioFormula
Current ratioCurrent assets / current liabilities
Quick ratio(Current assets − inventory) / current liabilities
Gross margin(Revenue − cost of sales) / revenue
Operating marginOperating profit / revenue
Debt-to-equityTotal debt / equity
Check your hand calculation

Current ratio 1.60 · quick ratio 0.90 · gross margin 30.0% · operating margin 10.0% · debt-to-equity 0.50.

Step 2. Prompt and run (15 minutes)

Copy this prompt into Gemini or Claude. Paste the code it gives you into Colab, upload the CSV and run it.

Starter prompt
I am an accounting student. I have a CSV file called ratios_data.csv with columns: company, year, revenue, cost_of_sales, operating_profit, current_assets, inventory, current_liabilities, total_debt, equity.

Write a Python script for Google Colab that:
1. Reads the CSV with pandas.
2. Calculates, for each company and year:
   - Current ratio = current_assets / current_liabilities
   - Quick ratio = (current_assets - inventory) / current_liabilities
   - Gross margin = (revenue - cost_of_sales) / revenue
   - Operating margin = operating_profit / revenue
   - Debt-to-equity = total_debt / equity
3. Prints a table rounded to 2 decimal places.
4. Does NOT guess when data is missing, zero or negative: print a warning naming the company, year and ratio instead.
Explain each step of the code in a short comment.

Step 3. Reconcile (10 minutes)

Compare the tool’s figures for Brindley 2023 with your hand calculation. Every difference needs a reason: rounding, a different definition, or an error.

Step 4. Hunt the traps (10 minutes)

The data contains three problems that a careless tool gets wrong. Find them. For each, note what the tool did (crashed, gave nonsense, or warned you) and re-prompt until it handles it properly.

Show the three traps
  • Brindley Engineering 2025: current liabilities of zero, almost certainly a data entry error. Dividing by zero returns infinity or crashes the tool.
  • Corve Retail 2024: inventory is missing. A tool that treats the blank as zero gives a quick ratio of 1.07, the same as the current ratio. It looks plausible, which is the danger.
  • Wrekin Software 2025: negative equity. A debt-to-equity of −5.21 looks like low gearing if nobody reads the sign. The ratio is meaningless.

Wrekin’s zero inventory in every year is not a trap. It is a software company, so its quick ratio equals its current ratio. Zero is not the same as missing.

Step 5. Extend (if you finish early)

Ask for a line chart of operating margin by company over time, or a two-sentence plain-English commentary on each company.

Discuss: Which error would you not have spotted without your hand calculation?

Practical twoAutomate an aged debtor review

sales_ledger.csv · 50 minutes

Kestrel Supplies Ltd has a year end of 31 March 2026. Its sales ledger holds 198 rows (customer, invoice number, invoice date, amount, amount paid), and it is messy, as real ledgers are. Produce an aged debtor report, the loss allowance and the year-end journal, and show that they reconcile.

Step 1. Find the problems, but don’t fix them yet (15 minutes)

The judgement stays with you. Ask the AI only to look.

Find-the-problems prompt
I have a CSV called sales_ledger.csv with columns customer, invoice_no, invoice_date, amount, amount_paid. Write Python for Google Colab that reads it and REPORTS any data quality problems, without changing anything. Check for: date formats, duplicate invoice numbers, credit notes, inconsistent customer names, and anything else unusual. Print the total outstanding (amount - amount_paid) at the start.

List each problem and decide how to treat it. Then prompt for the cleaning code, telling the AI your decisions and asking it to print the outstanding total after every step.

Show the four planted problems
  • Mixed date formats: 153 rows dd/mm/yyyy, 31 rows yyyy-mm-dd and 14 rows like “4 Apr 2025”. All are UK day-first. A tool that reads 03/04/2025 as 3 April is right; one that reads it as 4 March puts nine invoices in the future.
  • Duplicate invoice: INV-1131 (Ashdown Builders, £500.30) appears twice. Remove one.
  • Credit note sign: CN-0007 (Fenwick Garages, £1,200) is entered as a positive amount. It should be negative.
  • Customer spelled two ways: “Harrington and Cole Ltd” and “Harrington & Cole Ltd”. Standardise to one name.

Step 2. Age the balances (10 minutes)

Prompt for a report that ages each outstanding balance at 31 March 2026 by days since invoice date: 0–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ days, with totals by customer and by bucket.

Step 3. Calculate the loss allowance (10 minutes)

Use this provision matrix (IFRS 9 simplified approach). Apply each rate to its bucket total, not to the whole ledger. The rates are set for the exercise.

Days outstanding0–3031–6061–9090+
Expected loss rate1%3%10%40%

Step 4. Draft the journal (10 minutes)

The opening allowance for credit losses is £24,000. Journal only the movement needed to reach your closing allowance. What would the entry be if the required allowance were lower than £24,000?

Step 5. Reconcile (5 minutes)

The aged report total must equal the cleaned ledger’s outstanding balance. Show this line before you call the task done.

Show the headline answer

Cleaned outstanding balance £151,368.57, which the aged report matches. By bucket: 0–30 days £63,430.28; 31–60 £3,938.38; 61–90 £10,142.63; 90+ £73,857.28. Closing allowance £31,309.63.

Journal: Dr Impairment loss on trade receivables £7,309.63, Cr Allowance for credit losses £7,309.63. Had the allowance fallen, the entry would reverse: Dr Allowance, Cr Impairment loss.

Stuck? The same prompts work in Gemini in Google Sheets or Claude for Excel: ask for formulas instead of Python. See also the aged debtor exercise in Management Accounting with Claude for Excel.

CloseControls, ethics and the exit quiz

Red-team swap. Swap notebooks with the pair next to you and try to break their tool: change a figure, add a blank row, enter a future date. Report one weakness you found.

Discuss, one question per table:

  1. Confidentiality. Would you paste a client’s real sales ledger into a free AI tool? What changes with an enterprise licence?
  2. Accountability. The tool overstated the allowance by £40,000. Who signs off, and what evidence of review do you keep?
  3. Skills. If AI writes the code, what does a junior accountant still need to know?

As a table, write a five-bullet “AI use policy” for a small finance team.

Exit quiz

Four questions, on your phone. Show your tutor your score.
