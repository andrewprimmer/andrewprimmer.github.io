Teaching · Distance learning

ACCT 211 Principles of Accounting I

An introduction to financial accounting for distance learning: 14 lectures, each taught in six two-minute videos with a textbook reading and an interactive exercise set. It covers the accounting cycle, merchandising, internal control and cash, receivables, inventory, long-term assets and current liabilities.

Required textbook

Principles of Accounting, Volume 1: Financial Accounting

Mitchell Franklin, Patty Graybeal and Dixon Cooper. OpenStax, Rice University, 2019. ISBN 978-1-947172-68-5 (print), 978-1-947172-67-8 (digital).

Free to read online or download as a PDF from openstax.org, with a low-cost printed copy if you prefer paper. You do not need any other book. Every video on this site gives the textbook section and page numbers to read alongside it.

Page numbers are the printed page numbers in the PDF and print editions. In the web view, use the section number instead (for example, §4.2).

This course introduces how businesses report their operating, investing and financing activities to investors, creditors and others. It covers the basic financial statements, business transactions, the accounting cycle, forms of business organization, internal control, cash, receivables, inventories, long-term assets, depreciation and current liabilities. One small Marshall, Minnesota business runs through the whole course, so each new topic lands on accounts you already know.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook pages listed for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone, and lists the section and pages it covers.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set, then the textbook's Exercise Set A for that chapter.
Simulator Redwood River Books Keep the books of Redwood River Supply for its first year. Turn invoices, receipts, bank statements and payroll registers into journal entries, and watch every entry post to the T-accounts, the trial balance and the financial statements. Eight missions follow the course: opening the business, adjusting and closing, merchandising, FIFO, bank reconciliations, bad debts, buying a truck and running payroll. Open the simulator →
Every lecture has a textbook reading, six videos, a slide deck and an interactive exercise set, and the Redwood River Books simulator runs alongside the course. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Lectures

Part one · Foundations

  1. L1The role of accounting in societyWhat accounting is, who uses it, where double entry came from, who sets the rules, and careers in accounting. Read: Chapter 1, pp. 11–60.Week 1
  2. L2Introduction to financial statementsThe four statements, the elements they report, how they link, accrual accounting and the classified balance sheet. Read: Chapter 2, pp. 61–114.Week 2
  3. L3Analyzing and recording transactionsThe accounting equation, debits and credits, journalizing, posting to T-accounts and the trial balance. Read: Chapter 3, pp. 115–210.Week 3

Part two · The accounting cycle

  1. L4The adjustment processWhy we adjust, deferrals, accruals, depreciation and the adjusted trial balance. Read: Chapter 4, pp. 211–284.Week 4
  2. L5Completing the accounting cycleStatements from the adjusted trial balance, closing entries, the post-closing trial balance and the full cycle. Read: Chapter 5, pp. 285–364.Week 5
  3. L6Merchandising transactionsMerchandisers, perpetual and periodic systems, purchases, sales, discounts and the multi-step income statement. Read: Chapter 6, pp. 365–450.Week 6
  4. L7InventoryCost flow methods, perpetual and periodic costing, lower of cost and NRV, inventory errors and turnover. Read: Chapter 10, pp. 647–698.Week 7

Part three · Assets

  1. L8Fraud, internal controls and cashThe fraud triangle, SOX, COSO, controls over cash, petty cash and the bank reconciliation. Read: Chapter 8, pp. 531–576.Week 9
  2. L9Accounting for receivablesCredit sales, the direct write-off and allowance methods, the aging schedule and notes receivable. Read: Chapter 9, pp. 577–646.Week 10
  3. L10Long-term assets: acquisition and depreciationTangible and intangible assets, what goes into cost, three depreciation methods, partial years and revised estimates. Read: Chapter 11, pp. 699–744.Week 11
  4. L11Long-term assets: disposals, natural resources and intangiblesCapital versus revenue spending, disposals, impairment, depletion, amortization and reporting. Read: Chapter 11, pp. 699–744.Week 12

Part four · Liabilities and business forms

  1. L12Current liabilitiesWhat makes a liability current, notes payable, sales tax, unearned revenue, current debt and contingencies. Read: Chapter 12, pp. 745–808.Week 13
  2. L13Payroll liabilitiesGross to net pay, withholdings, employer payroll taxes, payroll entries, benefits and payroll forms. Read: Chapter 12, pp. 745–808.Week 14
  3. L14Forms of business organization and course reviewProprietorships, partnerships and corporations, equity under each, a historical annual report, and a course recap. Read: Chapters 14–15 (selected sections), pp. 860–937.Week 15
