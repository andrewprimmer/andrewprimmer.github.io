Microteach 13.1 · 1:13Gross pay to net pay

What the employee earns versus what lands in their bank account.

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

Redwood River's new warehouse worker earns a thousand dollars a week. But her paycheck says seven hundred fifty-eight fifty. Where did the rest go?

Gross pay is total earnings before anything is taken out: hours worked times the hourly rate, or a salary, plus any overtime or bonuses. Federal law requires overtime at one and a half times the regular rate for hours over forty a week for most hourly workers.

Net pay is what the employee actually takes home after every deduction.

Here's the week. Gross pay one thousand. Less federal income tax ninety, Minnesota income tax forty-five, Social Security sixty-two, Medicare fourteen fifty, and her share of health insurance thirty. Net pay: seven hundred fifty-eight fifty.

The amounts withheld don't belong to Redwood River. It holds them for the government and the insurer, so each one is a liability until it's paid over.

Quick check. Gross pay eight hundred, total deductions one ninety. What's net pay? Pause and pick.

It's B, six hundred ten.

Microteach 13.2 · 1:26Employee withholdings

Federal and Minnesota income tax, Social Security and Medicare (FICA).

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

Some deductions are required by law. Others the employee chooses. Here are the main ones on every US paycheck.

Required by law: federal income tax, based on the elections the employee makes on Form W-4. State income tax, which Minnesota requires employers to withhold. Social Security at six point two percent, on wages up to an annual limit. And Medicare at one point four five percent, on all wages.

Social Security and Medicare together are called FICA taxes, after the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. Combined, that's seven point six five percent. High earners also pay an extra Medicare tax on wages above two hundred thousand dollars.

Voluntary deductions are ones the employee chooses: their share of health insurance, contributions to a retirement plan like a four oh one k, union dues, and charitable gifts.

For our warehouse worker, FICA is seventy-six fifty: sixty-two for Social Security and fourteen fifty for Medicare.

Quick check. Gross pay is two thousand dollars, and no limits have been reached. What's the employee's total FICA? Pause and pick.

It's C, one fifty-three. Two thousand times seven point six five percent.

Microteach 13.3 · 1:28Employer payroll taxes

Matching FICA plus federal (FUTA) and state (SUTA) unemployment tax.

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

A thousand dollar wage costs Redwood River more than a thousand dollars. On top of the paycheck, the employer pays its own payroll taxes.

The employer matches the employee's Social Security and Medicare, dollar for dollar. It also pays federal unemployment tax, called FUTA, and state unemployment tax, called SUTA. Employees don't pay unemployment taxes.

FUTA is six percent on the first seven thousand dollars of each worker's wages a year. But employers who pay their state unemployment tax on time get a credit of up to five point four percent, so the usual effective rate is just point six percent.

For the warehouse worker's week: sixty-two for the Social Security match, fourteen fifty for Medicare, six for FUTA, and ten for state unemployment, using an illustrative one percent rate. Minnesota sets each employer's actual rate. Total employer payroll tax: ninety-two fifty.

So a one thousand dollar wage really costs Redwood River one thousand ninety-two fifty, before any benefits it provides.

Quick check. Which tax is paid by both the employee and the employer? Pause and pick.

It's C, Social Security. Medicare is too. Unemployment taxes fall on the employer, and income tax on the employee.

Microteach 13.4 · 1:16Recording a payroll

Two entries: one for wages and withholdings, one for employer taxes.

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

Every payroll takes two journal entries: one for the employee's pay, and one for the employer's taxes.

First, the wages. Debit wages expense for the full gross pay, one thousand. Credit a payable for each withholding: federal income tax ninety, state income tax forty-five, Social Security sixty-two, Medicare fourteen fifty, and health insurance thirty. Credit cash for the net pay, seven fifty-eight fifty.

Second, the employer's taxes. Debit payroll tax expense ninety-two fifty. Credit Social Security payable sixty-two, Medicare payable fourteen fifty, FUTA payable six and SUTA payable ten.

Notice Social Security payable now holds one twenty-four, half from the employee and half from the employer. The two halves are paid to the IRS together.

When the taxes are deposited, debit each payable and credit cash. The liabilities disappear and no new expense is recorded.

Quick check. In the first payroll entry, what's debited? Pause and pick.

It's B, wages expense for the full gross pay. The deductions are part of the employee's wage.

Microteach 13.5 · 1:14Benefits and compensated absences

Accrue vacation pay and benefits as employees earn them.

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

Pay isn't just wages. Vacation days, health insurance and retirement contributions are costs too, and they're earned as employees work.

Compensated absences are paid time off, like vacation, holidays and sick leave, that employees earn as they work. Under expense recognition, the cost belongs in the period the time is earned, not when it's taken.

Redwood River's worker earns one vacation day a month, worth two hundred dollars. Each month, debit vacation benefits expense and credit vacation benefits payable two hundred.

When she takes three days off in August, the payable is reduced by six hundred instead of recording a new expense. The cost was already recognized as she earned it.

Other benefits paid by the employer, like its share of health insurance, a four oh one k match and workers' compensation insurance, are recorded as expenses with matching liabilities until they're paid.

Quick check. When is the cost of a vacation day recognized? Pause and pick.

It's B, when it's earned. That's expense recognition at work.

Microteach 13.6 · 1:18Payroll forms

W-4, Form 941, Form 940 and the W-2.

Textbook: §12.5, p. 777 onward

Read the script

Running payroll means filing forms, on deadlines, with penalties if you're late. Here are the four every employer knows.

Four forms matter most. Form W-4 is completed by each new employee and tells the employer how much federal income tax to withhold. Form 941 is filed every quarter and reports wages, income tax withheld, and both halves of FICA. Form 940 reports federal unemployment tax once a year.

And Form W-2 shows each employee's wages and tax withheld for the year. It must reach employees, and the Social Security Administration, by January thirty-first.

Minnesota has its own filings too: state income tax withholding returns, and quarterly unemployment insurance reports.

Most small businesses, Redwood River included, use a payroll service to calculate, pay and file. But the employer stays legally responsible if anything goes wrong. You'll look at payroll systems again in ACCT 360.

Quick check. Which form reports each employee's annual wages and tax withheld? Pause and pick.

It's D, the W-2.
