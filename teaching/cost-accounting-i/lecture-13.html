Microteach 13.1 · 1:33Static budgets and variances

A variance is actual minus budget; favorable variances raise income, unfavorable ones lower it.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

Last month didn't go to plan. Variances tell managers by how much, and where to look.

A variance is the difference between an actual result and a budgeted amount. A favorable variance, marked F, increases operating income. An unfavorable variance, marked U, decreases it.

Prairie Gate's standard sixteen-foot utility trailer sells for $5,000. Its budgeted variable costs: five hundred pounds of steel at three dollars, a $1,000 kit of axles and lights, twenty-four hours of labor at thirty dollars, and variable overhead of ten dollars per labor hour. Contribution margin: $1,540.

The March static budget was forty trailers and $21,600 of operating income. Prairie Gate actually built and sold thirty-six, and earned $14,590. The static-budget variance is $7,010 unfavorable.

That single number isn't very useful. Some of the shortfall is because fewer trailers were sold; some is about prices and efficiency. The flexible budget separates them.

Quick check. Actual direct labor twenty-seven thousand nine hundred; budget twenty-eight eight hundred. Variance? Pause and pick.

It's A. Spending less than budget is favorable: $900 F. But we'll see that comparison isn't fair, because fewer trailers were made.

Microteach 13.2 · 1:30The flexible budget

The flexible budget uses budgeted prices and costs at the actual output, splitting the static-budget variance.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

To compare fairly, budget for the output actually achieved. That's the flexible budget.

A flexible budget uses budgeted selling prices and budgeted costs per unit, but the actual output: what the budget would have been if Prairie Gate had planned thirty-six trailers.

The flexible budget for thirty-six trailers shows revenue of $180,000, variable costs of $124,560, fixed costs of $40,000, and operating income of $15,440.

The sales-volume variance, flexible minus static, is $6,160 unfavorable: four fewer trailers times $1,540 of contribution. The flexible-budget variance, actual minus flexible, is only $850 unfavorable. That's what managers controlled through prices and costs.

These answer different questions. The sales-volume variance is mostly for marketing: why did we sell four fewer trailers? The flexible-budget variance is about prices and efficiency, which we break down next.

Quick check. Budgeted contribution is fifteen forty per trailer, and four fewer were sold. Sales-volume variance? Pause and pick.

It's A, $6,160 unfavorable.

Microteach 13.3 · 1:15Standard costs

Standards set the expected quantity and price of each input per unit of output.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

To break the flexible-budget variance down, you need standards: what each trailer should use, and what each input should cost.

A standard is a carefully determined benchmark: the quantity of each input a unit should use, and the price each input should cost. Prairie Gate's standard is five hundred pounds of steel at three dollars, and twenty-four labor hours at thirty dollars.

Ideal standards assume perfect conditions and are almost never met. Most companies use attainable standards: tight but achievable with efficient work, allowing for normal waste and downtime.

Standards come from engineering studies, past performance adjusted for improvements, benchmarks from other plants or companies, and supplier contracts for prices.

With standards, each cost's flexible-budget variance splits into a price variance, paying more or less per unit of input, and an efficiency variance, using more or less input than the standard allows for the actual output.

Quick check. Which standard most likely motivates welders? Pause and pick.

It's B. Attainable standards are challenging but reachable.

Microteach 13.4 · 1:42Direct materials variances

Price variance = (actual − budgeted price) × actual quantity; efficiency = (actual − standard quantity) × budgeted price.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

In March, Prairie Gate's steel cost $810 more than the flexible budget. Was it the price or the usage?

Prairie Gate used eighteen thousand nine hundred pounds of steel bought at two ninety a pound: $54,810. The flexible budget for thirty-six trailers allows eighteen thousand pounds at three dollars: $54,000. The variance is $810 unfavorable.

The price variance is the difference between the actual and budgeted price, times the actual quantity: ten cents a pound below standard, times eighteen thousand nine hundred pounds, is $1,890 favorable.

The efficiency variance is the difference between the actual quantity and the quantity allowed for actual output, times the budgeted price: nine hundred extra pounds at three dollars, $2,700 unfavorable.

Together: $1,890 favorable for price, $2,700 unfavorable for efficiency, $810 unfavorable in total. Cheaper steel, but more of it. Was it lower-grade steel that welded badly? That's the question for purchasing and production.

Quick check. Actual twenty-two hundred kilograms at five twenty; standard for actual output two thousand at five dollars. Efficiency variance? Pause and pick.

It's A. Two hundred extra kilograms at the budgeted five dollars: $1,000 unfavorable.

Microteach 13.5 · 1:34Direct labor variances

Labor price (rate) and efficiency variances use hours instead of pounds.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

Labor works the same way. The input is hours, and the price is the wage rate.

Welders and assemblers worked nine hundred hours at thirty-one dollars: $27,900. The flexible budget allows twenty-four hours per trailer, eight hundred sixty-four hours, at thirty dollars: $25,920. The variance is $1,980 unfavorable.

The labor price variance: a dollar an hour over standard, times nine hundred hours, $900 unfavorable. The efficiency variance: thirty-six extra hours at thirty dollars, $1,080 unfavorable.

In a standard costing system, work in process is charged at standard: $25,920. Unfavorable variances are debits; favorable ones would be credits. Wages payable records what was actually owed.

Variances interact. The favorable steel price and unfavorable steel and labor efficiency may share one cause: cheaper, lower-grade steel that took more welding time and created more scrap.

Quick check. Actual five twenty hours at twenty-nine dollars; standard for actual output five hundred hours at thirty. Price variance? Pause and pick.

It's A. One dollar less per hour times five hundred twenty hours: $520 favorable.

Microteach 13.6 · 1:25Using variances

Investigate variances that are large or recurring, look for shared causes, and use them to learn, not to blame.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 7.

Read the script

Variances are only useful if someone acts on them. Here's how to use them well.

Here's the whole $850 unfavorable flexible-budget variance: a favorable price variance of $3,600, offset by steel, labor, variable overhead and fixed cost overruns. Overhead variances are next lecture.

Not every variance needs investigating. Look at size, in dollars and as a percentage; trends, since a small variance that repeats every month matters; whether anyone can control it; and whether the benefit of investigating exceeds the cost.

Used for blame, variances encourage gaming: purchasing buys cheap steel to win a price variance and production pays for it. Used for learning, they bring purchasing and production together to find the best total cost.

Standards can also be compared with external benchmarks, the best plants or competitors, to see whether Prairie Gate's attainable standards are actually competitive.

Quick check. Purchasing shows a big favorable steel price variance, and production big unfavorable efficiency variances. What should management do? Pause and pick.

It's C. They may share one cause, and the total cost is what matters.
