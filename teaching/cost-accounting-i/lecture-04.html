Microteach 4.1 · 1:04Job costing or process costing?

Job costing tracks costs for distinct units; process costing averages costs over identical units.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

A custom trailer and a gallon of milk need different costing systems. Here's why.

Job costing is for distinct, custom products, like Prairie Gate's trailers, an audit or a car repair. Costs are collected for each job. Process costing is for masses of identical units, like milk or cereal, where cost is averaged over all units.

Prairie Gate builds every trailer to a customer's specification, so it uses job costing. Each trailer is a job with its own number.

Later we'll meet Redwood Valley Creamery, which turns milk into identical gallons, so it uses process costing. That's Lecture 8.

In practice many firms blend the two. A company that builds standard trailers with custom options might process-cost the base and job-cost the options.

Quick check. Which business should use job costing? Pause and pick.

It's C. Each house is distinct. The others make identical units.

Microteach 4.2 · 1:11Seven steps to cost a job

Trace direct costs, then allocate indirect costs with a rate based on a cost-allocation base.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

Costing a job follows seven steps. Learn them once and they work for trailers, audits and repairs.

One, identify the job. Two, trace its direct costs. Three, choose the base for allocating indirect costs. Four, identify the indirect cost pools. Five, compute the rate per unit of the base. Six, allocate indirect costs to the job. Seven, add it all up.

The cost-allocation base links indirect costs to jobs. Prairie Gate's factory is labor-intensive, so it uses direct labor hours: jobs that take more hours use more supervision, power and equipment.

The indirect cost rate is the indirect cost pool divided by the total quantity of the allocation base.

A simple system uses one plant-wide pool and rate. More refined systems use a pool for each department or activity, which you'll see in Lectures 6 and 7.

Quick check. After choosing the base and identifying the pool, what's next? Pause and pick.

It's B: compute the rate, pool divided by base.

Microteach 4.3 · 1:27Normal costing and budgeted rates

Normal costing applies overhead with a budgeted annual rate so job costs are known as work is done.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

Actual overhead isn't known until year-end. But Karen needs job costs now. The answer is normal costing.

Both systems trace actual direct costs. Actual costing allocates indirect costs at the actual rate, known only after the year ends. Normal costing uses a budgeted rate set at the start of the year, times the actual quantity of the base.

Prairie Gate budgets $1,728,000 of manufacturing overhead for 2027 and forty-eight thousand direct labor hours. Its budgeted overhead rate is thirty-six dollars per direct labor hour.

Why annual? Overhead is seasonal, with heating bills in winter. Volume swings month to month. Some bills arrive late. And a monthly rate would make an identical trailer cost more in January than in June.

Of course, budgets are rarely exact. The difference between overhead applied and overhead actually incurred is handled at year-end, in Lecture 5.

Quick check. Budgeted overhead nine hundred thousand; budgeted machine hours thirty thousand. A job uses twelve machine hours. How much overhead is applied? Pause and pick.

It's B. The rate is $30 an hour, times twelve: $360.

Microteach 4.4 · 1:05Source documents

Materials requisitions and labor time records feed each job cost record.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

Where do the numbers on a job come from? Three documents, usually digital now.

The job cost record collects every cost of one job. A materials requisition records materials issued to a specific job. A labor time record shows the hours each worker spent on each job.

Requisition twenty-two ninety-one issues forty lengths of steel tubing, two axles and four wheels to job four-eighteen: $5,400. A second requisition adds the flooring and lights later.

Today these are mostly digital. Workers scan a job barcode at each station, and materials are scanned out of the storeroom, so job costs update in real time.

Add up the job cost records for every unfinished job and you get the balance of the work-in-process control account.

Quick check. Which document shows a welder's hours on job four-eighteen? Pause and pick.

It's B, the labor time record.

Microteach 4.5 · 1:27Costing trailer #418

Job cost = direct materials + direct labor + overhead applied at the budgeted rate.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

Let's put the seven steps together and cost trailer four-eighteen, a twenty-four-foot livestock trailer.

Trailer four-eighteen used $6,200 of direct materials. Workers logged eighty hours at thirty dollars, $2,400. Overhead is applied at the budgeted rate of thirty-six dollars for each of those eighty hours, $2,880. Total job cost: $11,480.

The customer paid $14,500, so the job's gross margin is $3,020, or about twenty-one percent.

Karen uses job records like this to bid on future work. A similar trailer will take about eighty hours, and her price must also cover design, delivery and administration, which aren't in the job cost.

After each job, comparing actual hours and materials with the bid shows whether the estimates, or the welders, need attention.

Quick check. Job four-nineteen: direct materials forty-eight hundred, sixty labor hours at thirty dollars, overhead thirty-six an hour. Total cost? Pause and pick.

It's B. 4,800 plus 1,800 plus 2,160 is $8,760.

Microteach 4.6 · 1:26Job costing in services

Service firms cost each engagement with direct professional labor plus allocated support costs.

Reading: Horngren's Cost Accounting, Chapter 4.

Read the script

Job costing isn't just for factories. Accounting firms, hospitals and repair shops cost every job too.

In an accounting firm, the job is an engagement, the main direct cost is professional labor, and indirect costs are rent, administrative staff and technology.

Lyon and Company, a Marshall accounting firm, estimates four hundred professional hours at sixty dollars for Prairie Gate's audit: $24,000. Support costs are allocated at one hundred ten percent of professional labor, $26,400. The engagement will cost $50,400, and a twenty-five percent markup gives a bid of $63,000.

With no inventory, a service firm's job costs go straight to expense. But job costing still drives pricing, staffing and profitability analysis.

Firms track budgeted versus actual hours on every engagement. Overruns signal scope creep or a bid that was too low.

Quick check. An engagement uses a hundred fifty hours at seventy dollars, with support at ninety percent of labor. Total cost? Pause and pick.

It's B. Labor $10,500 plus support $9,450: $19,950.
