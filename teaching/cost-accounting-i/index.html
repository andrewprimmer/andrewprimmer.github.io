ACCT 413 Cost Accounting I

Cost flows, job and process costing, overhead, allocation and standard costs: the foundations of cost accounting for management decision-making, in 14 lectures and 84 two-minute videos, with a slide deck and an interactive exercise set for every lecture.

Required textbook

Horngren's Cost Accounting, 18th edition

Srikant M. Datar and Madhav V. Rajan. Pearson, 2025 (© 2026). eTextbook ISBN 9780135337936; MyLab Accounting optional.

The standard cost accounting text in the US, built around the theme of different costs for different purposes. ACCT 413 and ACCT 341 share this one book and split its 24 chapters between them with no overlap, so students buy it once and use it for both courses.

ACCT 413 reads Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 16–19. ACCT 341 Cost Accounting II reads the other fourteen.

A study of the basic development and application of accounting for management decision-making: cost flows in a manufacturing environment, with emphasis on job order and process cost systems, factory overhead analysis, activity-based costing, and cost allocation including joint products and byproducts. Prerequisite: ACCT 212 and ECON 201 or 202. Two fictional Marshall, Minnesota companies run through the course: Prairie Gate Trailers, which builds custom livestock and utility trailers one job at a time, and Redwood Valley Creamery, which makes cheddar in a continuous process with cream, skim and whey as joint products and byproducts. ACCT 341 Cost Accounting II uses the same textbook and carries on where this course stops.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook chapter for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set (112 questions in all), then the textbook's end-of-chapter problems.
Every lecture has a textbook chapter, six videos, a two-hour slide deck and an interactive exercise set. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Part one · Foundations

  1. L1The manager and management accountingHow management and cost accounting differ from financial accounting, strategy and the value chain, the five-step decision process, guidelines for management accountants, where cost accounting came from, and professional ethics. Read: Chapter 1.Week 1
  2. L2An introduction to cost terms and purposesCost objects, direct and indirect costs, variable and fixed costs, the three sectors and three inventories, inventoriable and period costs, prime and conversion costs, and how to treat overtime and idle time. Read: Chapter 2.Week 2
  3. L3Cost flows and the manufacturing statementsHow manufacturing costs flow from materials through work in process and finished goods to cost of goods sold, the schedule of cost of goods manufactured, the manufacturer's income statement, the journal entries, and three meanings of product cost. Read: Chapters 2 and 4.Week 3

Part two · Job costing and ABC

  1. L4Job costing with normal costingJob costing versus process costing, the seven-step approach, actual and normal costing, budgeted overhead rates, source documents and the job cost record, and job costing in service firms. Read: Chapter 4.Week 4
  2. L5Job costing: journal entries and year-end overheadJournal entries in a normal job costing system, underallocated and overallocated overhead, the adjusted allocation-rate, proration and write-off approaches, and department overhead rates. Read: Chapter 4.Week 5
  3. L6Activity-based costingBroad averaging and cost distortion, refining a costing system, activities and the cost hierarchy, building an ABC system, comparing ABC with traditional costing, and when ABC is worth it. Read: Chapter 5.Week 6
  4. L7Activity-based management and ABC beyond the factoryUsing ABC for pricing and product mix, cost reduction and value-added analysis, design decisions, ABC in merchandising and services, time-driven ABC, and activity-based budgeting. Read: Chapter 5.Week 7

Part three · Process costing

  1. L8Process costing: the weighted-average methodWhen process costing applies, equivalent units, the five-step approach, weighted-average physical and equivalent units, cost per equivalent unit and assigning costs, and journal entries between departments. Read: Chapter 18.Week 9
  2. L9Process costing: FIFO, transferred-in costs and hybrid systemsThe FIFO method, FIFO equivalent units, costs per equivalent unit and assignment, comparing FIFO with weighted average, transferred-in costs in a later department, and operation costing. Read: Chapter 18.Week 10
  3. L10Spoilage, rework and scrapDefinitions of spoilage, rework and scrap, normal and abnormal spoilage, spoilage in process costing, spoilage and rework in job costing, and accounting for scrap. Read: Chapter 19.Week 11

Part four · Allocation

  1. L11Allocating support-department and common costsSupport and operating departments, single-rate and dual-rate allocation, budgeted versus actual rates and quantities, the direct, step-down and reciprocal methods, and allocating common costs. Read: Chapter 16.Week 12
  2. L12Joint products and byproductsJoint costs and the splitoff point, the sales value at splitoff and physical-measure methods, the NRV and constant gross-margin methods, sell-or-process-further decisions, and accounting for byproducts. Read: Chapter 17.Week 13
