Microteach 10.1 · 1:16Basis and cost recovery

A business asset's basis includes everything paid to get it ready for use; that cost is recovered over time.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: IRS Pub. 946.

Read the script

When a business buys an asset that lasts for years, it can't usually deduct the cost at once. It recovers it over time. First, what is the cost?

An asset's cost basis is everything paid to acquire it and get it ready for use: the price, sales tax, delivery and installation.

Tangible property is depreciated, intangible property is amortized, and natural resources are depleted. Land is never depreciated, because it doesn't wear out.

Costs that better, restore or adapt an asset are capitalized. Routine repairs are deducted. And under the de minimis safe harbor, a small business can deduct items costing $2,500 or less each.

Every dollar of depreciation reduces the asset's adjusted basis. That matters when you sell, as we'll see in Lecture 11.

Quick check. Nora buys a printer for thirty-eight hundred dollars plus three hundred sales tax and one fifty delivery. What's her basis? Pause and pick.

It's C. Basis includes the tax and delivery: $4,250.

Microteach 10.2 · 1:24MACRS: classes and conventions

MACRS assigns each asset a recovery period, a method and a convention.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: IRS Pub. 946.

Read the script

The tax depreciation system is called MACRS. It doesn't ask how long your asset will really last. It tells you.

Cars, light trucks and computers are five-year property. Office furniture and most machinery are seven-year. Residential rental buildings are twenty-seven and a half years, and other buildings thirty-nine.

Personal property uses double-declining balance, switching to straight-line when that gives more. Buildings use straight-line. Salvage value is always ignored.

Conventions decide how much you get in the first and last year. Personal property assumes it was placed in service mid-year, the half-year convention. If more than forty percent of the year's purchases come in the last quarter, the mid-quarter convention applies. Buildings use mid-month.

Because of the half-year convention, five-year property is depreciated over six tax years: half a year at the start and half at the end.

Quick check. Nora buys six thousand dollars of equipment in March and five thousand in November, her only purchases. Which convention applies? Pause and pick.

It's B. $5,000 of $11,000 is forty-five percent in the fourth quarter, over forty percent, so mid-quarter.

Microteach 10.3 · 1:30Using the MACRS tables

Multiply the asset's basis by the table percentage for its class and year.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: IRS Pub. 946, Table A-1.

Read the script

In practice, nobody computes double-declining balance by hand. You use the IRS tables. Here's how.

The table gives a percentage for each year. Five-year property: twenty percent in year one, thirty-two in year two, nineteen point two in year three, eleven point five two in years four and five, and five point seven six in year six. Seven-year property starts at fourteen point two nine percent.

Nora bought $10,000 of cameras and lenses in 2025 and chose not to use bonus depreciation. Year one is twenty percent, $2,000. In 2026, year two, it's thirty-two percent: the $3,200 on her Schedule C. Adjusted basis falls to $4,800.

If you dispose of property before it's fully depreciated, take half of that year's amount under the half-year convention.

If Nora sells the cameras in 2027, the full year-three amount would be $1,920. She takes half: $960.

Quick check. A twenty thousand dollar seven-year machine is placed in service in 2026, with no bonus. What's year-one depreciation? Pause and pick.

It's A. Fourteen point two nine percent of $20,000 is $2,858.

Microteach 10.4 · 1:31Section 179 expensing

Section 179 lets a business expense up to $2,560,000 of equipment in 2026, limited to business income.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: Form 4562 instructions.

Read the script

Why depreciate over six years when you can deduct it all now? Section 179 lets businesses expense equipment immediately.

Section 179 lets a business elect to deduct the cost of qualifying equipment, machinery, software and certain building improvements in the year it's placed in service.

For 2026 the maximum is $2,560,000. It's reduced dollar for dollar once purchases of qualifying property exceed $4,090,000, so it targets small and mid-sized businesses.

The deduction can't exceed taxable income from active businesses. Any excess carries forward. So Section 179 can't create a loss.

In 2027, Nora buys a $14,000 professional drone kit and elects Section 179 for the full amount. Her business income is well above that, so she deducts all $14,000 that year, and there's nothing left to depreciate.

Quick check. A business with forty thousand dollars of taxable income elects sixty thousand of Section 179. What's deductible this year? Pause and pick.

It's B. The deduction is limited to $40,000 of business income. The other $20,000 carries forward.

Microteach 10.5 · 1:25Bonus depreciation

100% bonus depreciation is permanent for property acquired after January 19, 2025, and can create a loss.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: Form 4562 instructions.

Read the script

Bonus depreciation does what Section 179 does, but with no dollar cap and no income limit. The 2025 law made it permanent at one hundred percent.

The 2017 law allowed one hundred percent bonus depreciation, phasing down from 2023. The 2025 law restored one hundred percent permanently for property acquired after January nineteenth, 2025.

Section 179 has a dollar limit and an income limit, and you must elect it. Bonus depreciation has neither limit, can create a loss, and applies automatically unless you elect out for a whole class of property.

When both apply, take Section 179 first, then bonus on the remaining basis, then regular MACRS on anything left.

Why opt out? If you expect higher rates later, deductions are worth more then. Large deductions can also shrink your QBI deduction or waste credits.

Quick check. In 2026, a startup with a loss buys fifty thousand dollars of equipment. Which method gives a deduction that adds to its loss? Pause and pick.

It's B. Section 179 can't create a loss, but bonus depreciation can.

Microteach 10.6 · 1:36Buildings, listed property and intangibles

Buildings use straight-line and mid-month; listed property needs over 50% business use; Section 197 intangibles are amortized over 15 years.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 10. Free reading: IRS Pub. 946; Pub. 527.

Read the script

A few more cost recovery rules: for buildings, for property with personal use, and for intangibles.

Erik buys a rental duplex in July for $325,000, of which $50,000 is land. The building's $275,000 basis is depreciated straight-line over twenty-seven and a half years: $10,000 a year. Under the mid-month convention, July counts as half a month, so 2026 gets five and a half months: $4,583.

Listed property, mainly passenger cars, gets accelerated depreciation, Section 179 and bonus only if business use exceeds fifty percent. Otherwise it's straight-line. Passenger cars also face annual dollar caps, the so-called luxury auto limits.

Purchased intangibles, like goodwill, customer lists and non-compete agreements, are amortized straight-line over fifteen years.

Business start-up and organization costs can be deducted up to $5,000 each in the first year, phasing out above $50,000 of costs, with the rest amortized over a hundred and eighty months.

Quick check. Nora buys a retiring photographer's client list for thirty thousand dollars. What's the annual amortization? Pause and pick.

It's C. A customer list is a Section 197 intangible: $30,000 over fifteen years is $2,000 a year.
