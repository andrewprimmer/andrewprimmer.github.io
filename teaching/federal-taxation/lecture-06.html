Microteach 6.1 · 1:31Deductions for AGI

Above-the-line deductions include half of SE tax, HSA and IRA contributions, and student loan interest.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: Schedule 1 (Form 1040) instructions.

Read the script

Above-the-line deductions are the best kind: everyone who qualifies gets them, and they lower AGI. Here are the main ones.

The main deductions for AGI are half of self-employment tax, health insurance premiums for the self-employed, contributions to traditional IRAs and self-employed retirement plans, contributions to a health savings account, and up to $2,500 of student loan interest.

For 2026 the IRA limit is $7,500, plus $1,100 if you're fifty or older. Health savings account limits are $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage.

Why half of self-employment tax? Employers deduct their half of payroll tax as a business expense. Letting the self-employed deduct half puts them on a similar footing.

Suppose Nora also puts $3,000 into a family health savings account. With half her self-employment tax, that's $5,119 of deductions for AGI, saving $614 of income tax at twelve percent.

Quick check. Which is a deduction for AGI? Pause and pick.

It's C. Student loan interest is above the line. The others are itemized deductions.

Microteach 6.2 · 1:48New deductions: seniors and car loans

From 2025 to 2028, seniors deduct up to $6,000 and buyers of new US-assembled cars deduct up to $10,000 of loan interest.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: Schedule 1-A (Form 1040).

Read the script

The 2025 tax law created new deductions you can take whether or not you itemize. Two of them: one for seniors and one for car loans.

Each taxpayer aged sixty-five or older can deduct up to $6,000 from 2025 through 2028, on top of the standard or itemized deductions. It phases out by six percent of modified AGI above $75,000, or $150,000 on a joint return.

Gary, single and sixty-seven, has AGI of $23,500. His standard deduction with the over-sixty-five amount is $18,150. The $6,000 senior deduction takes his taxable income to zero.

The second: interest on a loan for a new personal car, truck or SUV whose final assembly was in the United States. Up to $10,000 a year is deductible from 2025 through 2028, phasing out above $100,000 of modified AGI, or $200,000 joint.

Jake buys a new, US-assembled pickup for personal use and pays $1,800 of loan interest in 2026. The Lindqvists are well under the $200,000 threshold, so they deduct it all, saving $216.

Quick check. Which car loan qualifies for the new deduction? Pause and pick.

It's B. The vehicle must be new, for personal use, with final assembly in the US. Business vehicle interest is deducted as a business expense instead.

Microteach 6.3 · 1:19Itemized deductions: medical expenses

Unreimbursed medical expenses are deductible only above 7.5% of AGI.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: IRS Pub. 502.

Read the script

Itemized deductions start with medical expenses. They're deductible, but only after a big floor.

Qualifying costs include payments to doctors, dentists and hospitals, prescription drugs, health insurance premiums you pay with after-tax dollars, and travel for medical care.

Only unreimbursed expenses above seven point five percent of AGI are deductible. Anything paid by insurance or from a health savings account doesn't count.

If the Lindqvists paid $10,000 of unreimbursed medical costs, the floor is seven point five percent of $116,281, or $8,721. Only $1,279 is deductible, and only if they itemize.

That's why timing matters. Scheduling elective procedures in the same year can push costs over the floor.

Quick check. AGI is sixty thousand dollars and unreimbursed medical costs are five thousand. What's deductible? Pause and pick.

It's B. The floor is $4,500, so $500 is deductible.

Microteach 6.4 · 1:33Itemized deductions: taxes and the SALT cap

State and local taxes are deductible up to $40,400 in 2026, less for very high incomes.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: Schedule A instructions.

Read the script

State and local taxes, or SALT, are deductible. But there's a cap, and it changed in 2025.

You can deduct state and local income taxes, or sales taxes instead if you choose, plus real estate taxes and personal property taxes based on value.

From 2018 to 2024, the deduction was capped at $10,000. The 2025 law raised it to $40,000 for 2025 and $40,400 for 2026, rising one percent a year, before returning to $10,000 in 2030.

For 2026 the cap shrinks by thirty percent of modified AGI above $505,000, but never below $10,000.

The Lindqvists paid $5,500 of Minnesota income tax and $3,200 of property tax. That's $8,700, well under the cap.

Quick check. A couple with modified AGI of five hundred fifty-five thousand dollars paid forty-five thousand of state taxes. What's their SALT cap? Pause and pick.

It's B. They're $50,000 over the threshold. Thirty percent of that is $15,000, and $40,400 minus $15,000 is $25,400.

Microteach 6.5 · 1:35Itemized deductions: interest and charity

Home mortgage interest on up to $750,000 of debt and gifts to charity are itemized deductions; from 2026, non-itemizers deduct cash gifts too.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: IRS Pub. 526; Pub. 936.

Read the script

The other big itemized deductions are mortgage interest and gifts to charity, and charity changed in 2026.

Mortgage interest is deductible on up to $750,000 of debt used to buy, build or substantially improve your main home and one other home. From 2026, mortgage insurance premiums count as interest again.

Gifts to qualified charities are deductible; gifts to individuals aren't. Cash gifts are limited to sixty percent of AGI, appreciated long-term property is deducted at fair value up to thirty percent, and any single gift of $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment.

From 2026, itemizers can deduct only the part of their gifts above half a percent of AGI. And non-itemizers get a new deduction for cash gifts: up to $1,000, or $2,000 on a joint return, on top of the standard deduction.

Maria Ortiz takes the head of household standard deduction and gives $1,500 to her church. As a non-itemizer filing alone, she can deduct $1,000, saving $120.

Quick check. Which gift is deductible? Pause and pick.

It's B. Gifts must go to a qualified charity. Gifts to individuals and political campaigns aren't deductible, and the value of your time never is.

Microteach 6.6 · 1:43Standard deduction or itemize?

Itemize only if itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction; most families now take the standard deduction.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 6. Free reading: Schedule A instructions.

Read the script

Every year, every taxpayer takes the larger of the standard deduction or their itemized deductions. Let's run the Lindqvists' numbers.

The Lindqvists' $3,000 of medical costs is below the floor. Taxes are $8,700 and mortgage interest is $9,800. That's $18,500 of itemized deductions, far less than the $32,200 standard deduction.

So they take the standard deduction. Since the 2017 law nearly doubled it, about nine out of ten filers do the same.

Families near the line can bunch: pay two years of charitable gifts, or schedule medical care, in one year to itemize, then take the standard deduction the next.

And remember what you keep either way: the deductions for AGI, the business income deduction, the new senior, tips, overtime and car loan deductions, and from 2026, the charitable deduction for non-itemizers.

Quick check. A single filer has nine thousand dollars of state taxes, six thousand of mortgage interest and two thousand of gifts, with AGI of a hundred thousand. Should she itemize? Pause and pick.

It's B. Half a percent of $100,000 is $500, so $1,500 of the gifts count. $9,000 plus $6,000 plus $1,500 is $16,500, just above $16,100.
