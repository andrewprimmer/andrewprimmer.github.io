Microteach 4.1 · 1:55The individual tax formula

Gross income less deductions gives taxable income; tax less credits, plus other taxes, less payments gives the amount due.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: Form 1040 and instructions.

Read the script

Every Form 1040 follows the same formula. Learn it once, and every topic in this course has a place to land.

Start with gross income. Subtract deductions for AGI to reach adjusted gross income. Subtract the larger of the standard deduction or itemized deductions, and the qualified business income deduction, to reach taxable income.

Jake earns $88,000, Nora's photography business nets $30,000, and they have $400 of interest. After deducting half of Nora's self-employment tax, their AGI is $116,281. Less the $32,200 standard deduction and a $5,576 business income deduction, taxable income is $78,505.

Tax on $78,505 is $8,925. Two child tax credits cut it by $4,400. Add Nora's self-employment tax of $4,239 for total tax of $8,764. They've already paid $9,000 through withholding and estimates, so they get a $236 refund.

Notice the difference. A deduction reduces income, so it's worth the deduction times your marginal rate. A credit reduces tax dollar for dollar.

Quick check. At a twelve percent marginal rate, which saves the Lindqvists more: a thousand-dollar deduction or a two hundred dollar credit? Pause and pick.

It's B. The deduction saves twelve percent of $1,000, which is $120. The credit saves the full $200.

Microteach 4.2 · 1:48For AGI, from AGI, and the standard deduction

Deductions for AGI are more valuable because AGI sets many floors and phase-outs.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: IRS Pub. 501.

Read the script

Deductions come in two kinds: above the line and below it. Where a deduction lands changes what it's worth.

Deductions for AGI, called above-the-line, include half of self-employment tax and contributions to IRAs and health savings accounts. Deductions from AGI, below the line, are the standard deduction or itemized deductions.

Above-the-line deductions are better, because AGI is the yardstick for many other rules. Medical expenses are deductible only above seven and a half percent of AGI, and many credits phase out as AGI rises.

For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for joint filers and $24,150 for heads of household. Taxpayers sixty-five or older, or blind, get an extra amount: $2,050 if unmarried, or $1,650 each if married.

Someone who can be claimed as a dependent gets a smaller standard deduction: the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450. That stops parents shifting investment income to children tax-free.

Quick check. Ruth, Jake's mother, is sixty-eight, single and not blind. What's her 2026 standard deduction? Pause and pick.

It's C. $16,100 plus the $2,050 extra for an unmarried taxpayer over sixty-five gives $18,150. The new senior deduction comes on top, and we'll meet it in Lecture 6.

Microteach 4.3 · 1:34Who is a qualifying child?

A qualifying child passes the relationship, age, residence and support tests.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: IRS Pub. 501.

Read the script

Dependents unlock credits and filing statuses worth thousands. The first kind of dependent is a qualifying child. Here are the tests.

Relationship: your child, stepchild, foster child, sibling, or a descendant of one. Age: under nineteen, or under twenty-four if a full-time student. Residence: lived with you over half the year. Support: the child didn't pay over half of their own support.

Temporary absences for school, illness or military service count as time living at home. So Sam Ortiz, twenty, in an SMSU dorm, still lives with his mother for this test.

Check Sam. He's Maria's son. He's twenty and a full-time student. The dorm is a temporary absence. He earns $9,000 waiting tables, but Maria provides $14,000 of his support. He's her qualifying child.

If two people can claim the same child, a parent wins. Between two parents, the child goes to the one they lived with longer, and if that's equal, to the parent with the higher AGI.

Quick check. Leo, fifteen, lives at home and earns twenty thousand dollars a year modeling, which he saves. Is he his parents' qualifying child? Pause and pick.

It's B. There's no income limit for a qualifying child. The test is whether he paid more than half of his own support, and saving the money doesn't count as support.

Microteach 4.4 · 1:32Who is a qualifying relative?

A qualifying relative passes the relationship or household, gross income and support tests.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: IRS Pub. 501.

Read the script

Parents, grandparents, even a friend who lives with you can be a dependent. These are qualifying relatives.

A qualifying relative can't be anyone's qualifying child. They must be a relative, or a member of your household all year. Their gross income must be under $5,300 for 2026. And you must provide more than half of their support.

Close relatives, like parents, grandparents, aunts and uncles, qualify by relationship alone. They don't have to live with you.

Ruth lives on her own in Tracy. Her non-taxable Social Security isn't gross income. Her IRA withdrawals of $4,800 are under the limit. Jake pays $11,000 of her $18,000 living costs, more than half. So she's his qualifying relative.

If several siblings together pay more than half of a parent's support, but none pays over half alone, they can sign a multiple support agreement. Any one who paid more than ten percent can claim the parent.

Quick check. Jake's adult cousin lives with them all year, earns four thousand dollars, and Jake supports him. Is he a qualifying relative? Pause and pick.

It's B. Cousins aren't on the relationship list, but anyone who lives with you all year as a member of your household passes, and his income is under $5,300.

Microteach 4.5 · 1:20The five filing statuses

Marital status on December 31 decides between joint, separate, surviving spouse, head of household and single.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: IRS Pub. 501.

Read the script

Filing status sets your standard deduction and your tax brackets. There are five, and the choice starts with one question: were you married on December thirty-first?

The five statuses are married filing jointly, married filing separately, qualifying surviving spouse, head of household and single.

Marital status is decided on the last day of the year. Marry on December thirty-first and you're married for the whole year. Divorce is final by then, and you're unmarried for the whole year.

Most married couples file jointly, which gives the widest brackets. Filing separately can protect a spouse from the other's tax debts, but it loses many credits and deductions.

In the year a spouse dies, the survivor can still file jointly. For the next two years, a survivor who keeps a home for a dependent child can file as a qualifying surviving spouse, keeping the joint brackets and standard deduction.

Quick check. Kari and Erik married on December twenty-eighth, 2026. What filing statuses can they use for 2026? Pause and pick.

It's B. Married on December thirty-first means married for the year, so they file jointly or separately.

Microteach 4.6 · 1:31Head of household

An unmarried taxpayer who pays over half the cost of a home for a qualifying person can file as head of household.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 4. Free reading: IRS Pub. 501.

Read the script

Head of household gives single parents a bigger standard deduction and wider brackets than single status. Here's who qualifies.

You must be unmarried at year-end, or considered unmarried. You must pay more than half the cost of keeping up a home. And a qualifying person, usually a qualifying child, must live there for more than half the year.

One exception: a dependent parent doesn't have to live with you. You qualify if you pay more than half the cost of keeping up your parent's home.

Maria Ortiz is divorced and keeps a home for Sam. Her AGI is $85,000, and after the head of household standard deduction her taxable income is $60,850. Single brackets would charge $8,099. Head of household brackets charge $6,948, a saving of $1,151, on top of the bigger standard deduction.

A married person can be considered unmarried if their spouse didn't live in the home for the last six months of the year, and they paid over half the cost of keeping up a home for their dependent child.

Quick check. Maria's sister is single and pays all the costs of their dependent mother's apartment. What filing status can she use? Pause and pick.

It's B. A dependent parent needn't live with you, so paying over half the cost of her home qualifies the sister as head of household.
