ACCT 414 Federal Tax I

Theory and principles of the federal income tax for individuals, in 14 lectures and 84 two-minute videos, with a slide deck and an interactive exercise set for every lecture. Every figure is for tax year 2026, including the changes made by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

Required textbook

McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, 2027 release

Brian C. Spilker, Benjamin C. Ayers, John A. Barrick, Troy Lewis, John Robinson, Connie Weaver and Ronald G. Worsham. McGraw Hill, Evergreen release for 2027 (covers 2026 tax law).

The most widely adopted individual tax text in the US. Its fourteen chapters match this course's fourteen lectures one for one, so each lecture's reading is simply its chapter. The book is updated every year: use the 2027 release, which reflects the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Connect access is optional unless your instructor assigns homework through it.

Free companion: IRS Publication 17, Your Federal Income Tax, and the IRS publications listed under each video. Every dollar figure on this site is for tax year 2026.

This course covers the theory and principles involved in computing federal income tax for individuals: the tax system and the IRS, tax planning, the individual tax formula, gross income and exclusions, deductions, investments, the tax computation and credits, business income and property, compensation, retirement savings and home ownership. Prerequisite: ACCT 212. One Marshall, Minnesota family runs through the course: Jake and Nora Lindqvist, their children Ava and Leo, Jake's mother Ruth, and Nora's photography business. Their 2026 return is built up lecture by lecture and completed in the last video.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook chapter for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone, and lists a free IRS publication to go with it.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set (112 questions in all), then the textbook's end-of-chapter problems.
Every lecture has a textbook chapter, six videos, a two-hour slide deck and an interactive exercise set. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Lectures

Part one · The tax system

  1. L1An introduction to taxWhat a tax is, how to measure tax rates, progressive and regressive structures, the taxes Americans pay, where the income tax came from, and what makes a good tax. Read: Chapter 1.Week 1
  2. L2Tax compliance, the IRS and tax authoritiesWho must file and when, the statute of limitations, how audits are selected and resolved, the sources of tax law, and the penalties and standards that govern taxpayers and preparers. Read: Chapter 2.Week 2
  3. L3Tax planning strategies and their limitsThe three basic planning strategies: timing, income shifting and conversion, how the time value of money drives them, and the doctrines that stop plans going too far. Read: Chapter 3.Week 3

Part two · The individual tax formula

  1. L4Individual income tax overview, dependents and filing statusThe individual tax formula from gross income to tax due, deductions for and from AGI, qualifying children and relatives, and the five filing statuses. Read: Chapter 4.Week 4
  2. L5Gross income and exclusionsThe all-inclusive definition of gross income, when and to whom income is taxed, common types of income, Social Security benefits, and the main exclusions and fringe benefits. Read: Chapter 5.Week 5
  3. L6Individual deductionsDeductions for AGI, the new senior and car loan deductions, itemized deductions for medical costs, taxes, interest and charity, and the choice between itemizing and the standard deduction. Read: Chapter 6.Week 6
  4. L7InvestmentsInterest and dividends, basis and holding period, capital gains rates, netting gains and losses, wash sales, investment interest, the net investment income tax and passive losses. Read: Chapter 7.Week 7
  5. L8Individual income tax computation and tax creditsComputing the regular tax, the kiddie tax and AMT, payroll and self-employment taxes, the child and dependent care credits, education credits, the EITC and paying tax during the year. Read: Chapter 8.Week 9

Part three · Business and property

  1. L9Business income, deductions and accounting methodsThe ordinary and necessary test, limits on business deductions, mixed-use expenses such as the home office, hobbies, accounting periods and methods, and the qualified business income deduction. Read: Chapter 9.Week 10
  2. L10Property acquisition and cost recoveryCost basis and capital versus repair, MACRS recovery periods and conventions, the MACRS tables, Section 179, bonus depreciation, real property, listed property and amortization. Read: Chapter 10.Week 11
  3. L11Property dispositionsAmount realized and gain, the character of gains, Section 1245 and 1250 recapture, Section 1231 netting, like-kind exchanges, involuntary conversions and installment sales. Read: Chapter 11.Week 12

Part four · Compensation, retirement and the home

  1. L12CompensationSalary and withholding, the new deductions for tips and overtime, employees versus contractors, stock options, restricted stock and the Section 83(b) election, and accountable plans. Read: Chapter 12.Week 13
  2. L13Retirement savings and deferred compensationDefined benefit and defined contribution plans, traditional and Roth 401(k)s, distributions and penalties, IRAs, plans for the self-employed, and nonqualified deferred compensation. Read: Chapter 13.Week 14
  3. L14Tax consequences of home ownership, and course reviewThe exclusion of gain on a home sale, partial exclusions, mortgage interest and points, property taxes at closing, renting out a home or vacation home, and a full return to close the course. Read: Chapter 14.Week 15
