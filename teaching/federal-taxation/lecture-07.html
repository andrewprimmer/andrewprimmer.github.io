Microteach 7.1 · 1:22Interest and dividends

Interest is ordinary income unless it is municipal; qualified dividends get capital gains rates.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: IRS Pub. 550.

Read the script

Investment income comes as interest, dividends and gains. Each is taxed differently. Start with interest and dividends.

Interest from banks and corporate bonds is ordinary income. Treasury interest is federally taxable but exempt from state income tax. Municipal bond interest is federally exempt, and Minnesota taxes interest on bonds from other states.

Most dividends from US companies are qualified dividends, taxed at the lower capital gains rates, if you've held the shares for more than sixty days in the hundred and twenty-one days around the ex-dividend date. Other dividends are ordinary income.

If Jake were in the twenty-two percent bracket, $1,000 of ordinary dividends would cost $220. As qualified dividends, taxed at fifteen percent, they cost $150.

Why the break? Corporate profits are taxed at twenty-one percent before they're paid out, then again as dividends. The lower dividend rate softens that double tax.

Quick check. Which interest does Minnesota exempt but the IRS taxes? Pause and pick.

It's B. States can't tax interest on federal debt, but it's fully taxable federally.

Microteach 7.2 · 1:27Basis and holding period

Gain is amount realized less basis; property held more than one year is long-term.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: IRS Pub. 551.

Read the script

To compute a gain you need two numbers: what you got and what you paid. And the calendar decides the rate.

Gain or loss is the amount realized minus your basis. For purchased shares, basis is the price plus commissions.

Bought property has a cost basis. Inherited property gets a basis equal to its value at death, called a step-up, and is always long-term. A gift carries over the donor's basis and holding period.

Ruth bought shares in 2015 for $2,000. Now they're worth $10,000. If she gives them to Leo, his basis is her $2,000, and he'll owe tax on $8,000 when he sells. If he inherited them, his basis would be $10,000 and the gain would disappear.

Property held more than one year is long-term. Shares bought on March third, 2026 are long-term if sold on or after March fourth, 2027.

Quick check. Jake inherits shares worth fifteen thousand dollars that his father bought for four thousand, and sells them a month later for fifteen thousand five hundred. What's his gain? Pause and pick.

It's B. His basis steps up to $15,000, so the gain is $500, and inherited property is always long-term.

Microteach 7.3 · 1:45Capital gains rates

Long-term gains are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on taxable income; short-term gains at ordinary rates.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: Schedule D instructions.

Read the script

Long-term capital gains get their own rate schedule. Some families pay nothing at all on them.

In 2026, long-term gains and qualified dividends are taxed at zero percent while taxable income stays under $49,450 for a single filer or $98,900 for a joint return. Then fifteen percent, up to $545,500 single or $613,700 joint, and twenty percent above.

A few gains have special maximum rates: twenty-eight percent for collectibles like coins and art, and twenty-five percent for depreciation taken on real estate. Short-term gains are taxed at ordinary rates.

The Lindqvists' taxable income is $78,505. If they sell shares at a $10,000 long-term gain, taxable income rises to $88,505, still below $98,900. The whole gain is taxed at zero percent.

The key idea is stacking. Ordinary income fills the brackets first, and gains sit on top. Only the part of the gain above the threshold moves to fifteen percent.

Quick check. A single filer has sixty thousand dollars of ordinary taxable income and a five thousand dollar long-term gain. What's the tax on the gain? Pause and pick.

It's B. Ordinary income alone is above $49,450, so the whole gain is taxed at fifteen percent: $750.

Microteach 7.4 · 1:31Netting gains and losses

Net short-term and long-term results separately, then together; deduct up to $3,000 of net capital loss a year.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: Schedule D instructions.

Read the script

Most investors have gains and losses in the same year. The netting rules decide what's taxed and at what rate.

First, net short-term gains and losses together. Then net long-term gains and losses. If one result is a gain and the other a loss, net them against each other.

Jake has a $3,000 short-term gain and a $5,000 short-term loss: net short-term loss, $2,000. A $6,000 long-term gain and a $1,000 long-term loss: net long-term gain, $5,000. Together, a net long-term gain of $3,000, taxed at the capital gains rate.

If the final result is a loss, you can deduct up to $3,000 a year against ordinary income, $1,500 if married filing separately. The rest carries forward indefinitely, keeping its short- or long-term character.

Erik's net capital loss is $10,000. He deducts $3,000 in 2026 and carries $7,000 to 2027.

Quick check. Net short-term gain four thousand; net long-term loss nine thousand. What happens? Pause and pick.

It's B. The net loss is $5,000, all long-term. Deduct $3,000 and carry $2,000 forward as a long-term loss.

Microteach 7.5 · 1:17Wash sales and related-party losses

A loss is disallowed if you buy the same security within 30 days, or sell to a related party.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: IRS Pub. 550.

Read the script

Selling shares at a loss in December to cut your tax bill is fine. Buying them straight back isn't. Here are the loss traps.

A wash sale happens when you sell shares at a loss and buy substantially identical shares within thirty days before or after the sale. The loss is disallowed.

Jake sells shares that cost $8,000 for $5,000 on December tenth, then buys them back on December twenty-eighth for $5,200. The $3,000 loss is disallowed, but it isn't lost: it's added to the basis of the new shares, making it $8,200.

So a wash sale defers the loss rather than destroying it. The old holding period carries over as well.

Losses on sales to related parties, like a parent, child, sibling, or a company you control, are disallowed outright. The buyer can use the disallowed loss only to reduce a later gain when they sell.

Quick check. Jake sells shares at a two thousand dollar loss to his brother Erik. Can Jake deduct it? Pause and pick.

It's B. Siblings are related parties, so the loss is disallowed.

Microteach 7.6 · 1:40Investment interest, NIIT and passive losses

Investment interest is limited to investment income, high earners pay a 3.8% NIIT, and passive losses offset only passive income.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 7. Free reading: Form 8960; IRS Pub. 925.

Read the script

Three more investment rules, each aimed at a different kind of tax shelter.

Interest on money borrowed to buy investments is an itemized deduction, but only up to your net investment income. The excess carries forward. Qualified dividends and long-term gains count only if you elect to tax them at ordinary rates.

The net investment income tax adds three point eight percent on interest, dividends, gains, rents and passive income, to the extent modified AGI exceeds $200,000 single or $250,000 joint. These thresholds aren't indexed.

A passive activity is a business you don't materially participate in, and most rental real estate. Passive losses can only offset passive income. Unused losses wait until you have passive income or sell the activity.

One exception: if you actively participate in a rental, you can deduct up to $25,000 of rental losses against other income. It phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of AGI.

Quick check. Erik, with AGI of two hundred eighty thousand, has a fifteen thousand dollar loss on a rental he manages. How much can he deduct? Pause and pick.

It's C. His AGI is above $150,000, so the $25,000 exception is gone. The loss is suspended until he has passive income or sells the property.
