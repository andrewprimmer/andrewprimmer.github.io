Microteach 5.1 · 1:20What counts as gross income?

Gross income is all realized income from whatever source, unless the Code excludes it.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 525.

Read the script

The Code doesn't list what's taxable. It says everything is, unless something says otherwise. Here's how gross income works.

Section 61 defines gross income as all income from whatever source derived. That's deliberately broad: wages, tips, business profits, rent, prizes, even illegal income.

Income is taxed when it's an accession to wealth, when it's been realized through a transaction, when it isn't just getting your own money back, and when no provision excludes or defers it.

Realization matters. If Jake's shares rise by $5,000 this year, he's wealthier, but there's no income until he sells. That's why gains can be deferred for decades.

And getting back your own money isn't income. If Jake sells shares that cost $3,000 for $5,000, only the $2,000 gain is income. The $3,000 is a return of capital.

Quick check. Which of these is gross income? Pause and pick.

It's B. The prize is realized and makes Nora wealthier. The house hasn't been sold, the loan repayment is a return of capital, and deposits are Leo's own money.

Microteach 5.2 · 1:33When, and to whom, income is taxed

Cash-method taxpayers report income when received or constructively received; income is taxed to whoever earns it.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 538.

Read the script

Two questions follow every dollar of income. In which year is it taxed? And who pays the tax on it?

Almost all individuals use the cash method: income is taxed when it's received. Larger businesses use the accrual method: income is taxed when it's earned, whether or not the cash has arrived.

Cash-method taxpayers still face rules. Income available to you is constructively received. A check or property counts as cash. Money you receive under a claim of right is taxed now, even if you might have to repay it. And prepaid income is generally taxed when received.

On the question of who, income from services is taxed to the person who did the work. Income from property is taxed to whoever owns the property. To shift investment income, you have to give away the property itself.

Nine states, including California and Texas, use community property rules, which split most income earned during marriage equally between spouses. Minnesota is a common-law state, so each spouse owns what they earn.

Quick check. On December thirty-first, a client tells Nora her twelve hundred dollar check is at the front desk. She picks it up on January second. When is it income? Pause and pick.

It's A, 2026. The check was available to her without restriction, so she constructively received it on December thirty-first.

Microteach 5.3 · 1:27Common types of income

Wages, tips, business and rental income, investment income, prizes, unemployment and forgiven debt are all taxable.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 525.

Read the script

Most income is easy to spot. Some is less obvious. Here's a tour of the common types, including a few that surprise people.

Wages, bonuses and tips are taxable, as are business, rental and farm profits, interest, dividends and capital gains. So is unemployment compensation.

Less obvious: prizes and gambling winnings are taxable. If you trade services, the value of what you receive is income. If a lender forgives a debt, the forgiven amount is generally income. And illegal income is taxable too, which is how Al Capone went to prison in 1931.

Alimony depends on the date of the divorce. For divorces finalized before 2019, the payer deducts alimony and the recipient includes it. For divorces in 2019 or later, neither does. Child support is never income or deductible.

Income is taxable whether or not you get a W-2 or a 1099. Cash jobs, barter and payments through apps must all be reported.

Quick check. Nora photographs a plumber's wedding in exchange for fifteen hundred dollars of plumbing work. What does she report? Pause and pick.

It's B. Barter income is the fair value of what she receives: $1,500.

Microteach 5.4 · 1:28Taxing Social Security benefits

Up to 85% of Social Security benefits are taxable, depending on provisional income.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 915.

Read the script

Are Social Security benefits taxable? Sometimes. It depends on a figure called provisional income.

Provisional income is your AGI without the benefits, plus tax-exempt interest, plus half of your Social Security benefits.

For a single filer, benefits start to be taxable above $25,000 of provisional income, and up to eighty-five percent can be taxable above $34,000. For joint filers the thresholds are $32,000 and $44,000. These thresholds have never been indexed for inflation.

Gary is single, with a $20,000 pension and $24,000 of Social Security. His provisional income is $32,000. That's between the two thresholds, so the taxable amount is the lesser of half his benefits or half the excess over $25,000: $3,500.

Ruth's provisional income is only $16,800, her IRA withdrawals plus half her benefits, so none of her Social Security is taxable.

Quick check. A single retiree has provisional income of twenty thousand dollars. How much of her benefits is taxable? Pause and pick.

It's A, none. She's below the $25,000 threshold.

Microteach 5.5 · 1:30Exclusions

Gifts, inheritances, life insurance, municipal interest and qualified scholarships are excluded from gross income.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 525; Pub. 970.

Read the script

Exclusions are income the Code lets you leave out entirely. Here are the ones you'll meet most often.

Gifts and inheritances are excluded, though income they later earn is taxable. So are life insurance proceeds paid on a death, interest on state and local bonds, damages for physical injury or sickness, and most gain on selling your home, which we'll cover in Lecture 14.

Scholarships are excluded only to the extent they pay for tuition, required fees, books and supplies. Amounts for room and board are taxable. And payment for services, like a teaching assistantship, is wages.

Sam gets a $6,000 scholarship. $5,200 covers tuition and fees and $500 his required books, so $5,700 is excluded. The remaining $300 goes toward his dorm room, and that's taxable income.

One more: US citizens who live and work abroad can exclude up to $132,900 of foreign earned income in 2026.

Quick check. Ruth inherits shares worth forty thousand dollars and then receives six hundred dollars of dividends on them. What's her gross income? Pause and pick.

It's B. The inheritance is excluded, but the dividends it earns are taxable: $600.

Microteach 5.6 · 1:28Fringe benefits

Many employer-provided benefits are tax-free, which makes them worth more than equal cash pay.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 5. Free reading: IRS Pub. 15-B.

Read the script

A dollar of health insurance from your employer can be worth more than a dollar of salary. Here's why fringe benefits matter.

Common tax-free fringes include employer health insurance and group-term life insurance up to $50,000, no-additional-cost services like an airline's empty seats, employee discounts, work tools, small de minimis perks, parking and transit up to $340 a month, and dependent care assistance up to $7,500 in 2026.

Jake's employer pays $6,000 of his family's health insurance. If he got that as salary instead, he'd pay twelve percent income tax and seven point six five percent payroll tax: $1,179. As a fringe benefit, it's tax-free.

To be tax-free, many fringe benefits must be offered to employees broadly, not just to owners and highly paid executives.

A cafeteria plan lets employees choose between cash and benefits without being taxed on the choice. Health flexible spending accounts are capped at $3,400 for 2026.

Quick check. Which benefit is taxable to Jake? Pause and pick.

It's C. Cash and gift cards are never de minimis, however small. The life cover, the turkey and the parking are tax-free.
