Microteach 1.1 · 1:15What makes something a tax?

A tax is a required payment to government that buys no specific benefit in return.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1. Free reading: IRS Pub. 17, introduction.

Read the script

Taxes pay for roads, schools and defense. But not every payment you make to a government is a tax. Here's the test.

A tax is a payment required by a government that isn't tied to any specific benefit you receive. You pay it whether or not you use the roads it builds.

So a tax has three features. It's required by law. It's paid to a government. And it's unrequited: you get no particular good or service in exchange.

Test some payments. Federal income tax is a tax. A state park pass is a fee: it buys entry. A speeding ticket is a fine. A cigarette tax is still a tax, even though it also discourages smoking.

Taxes do two jobs. They raise the revenue government needs, and they shape behavior. Sin taxes discourage things, and tax credits reward things, like saving for retirement or paying for college.

Quick check. Which of these is a tax? Pause and pick.

It's B. Sales tax is required and buys nothing specific. The park pass and tuition are fees for a service, and the parking fine is a penalty.

Microteach 1.2 · 1:43Marginal, average and effective rates

The marginal rate taxes the next dollar; the average rate divides total tax by taxable income.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1. Free reading: Form 1040 instructions, 2026 tax rate schedules.

Read the script

Someone tells you they're in the twenty-two percent bracket. That doesn't mean they pay twenty-two percent of their income. Here's why.

The marginal tax rate is the rate on your next dollar of taxable income. The US income tax works in brackets, and each bracket's rate applies only to the income inside it.

Take a single filer with $60,000 of taxable income in 2026. The first $12,400 is taxed at ten percent, the next $38,000 at twelve percent, and only the last $9,600 at twenty-two percent. Total tax: $7,912.

Her marginal rate is twenty-two percent. Her average rate, tax divided by taxable income, is 13.2 percent. Her effective rate divides tax by all her income, including the $16,100 standard deduction: about 10.4 percent.

So a raise never leaves you worse off: only the dollars above the threshold are taxed at the higher rate. Use the marginal rate for decisions, and the average rate to describe the overall burden.

Quick check. A single filer's taxable income rises from fifty thousand to fifty-one thousand dollars. How much more income tax? Pause and pick.

It's B. The first $400 is still in the twelve percent bracket, so $48. The other $600 is taxed at twenty-two percent, $132. Total $180.

Microteach 1.3 · 1:36Progressive, proportional and regressive

Tax structures are judged by how the average rate changes as the tax base grows.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1.

Read the script

Some taxes take a bigger share from people with more income, some the same share, and some a smaller share. Here are the three structures.

A progressive tax has an average rate that rises as the tax base rises. A proportional, or flat, tax charges one rate on every dollar. A regressive tax has an average rate that falls as the base rises.

The federal income tax is progressive. The Medicare tax is proportional. Social Security tax stops at $184,500 of wages in 2026, so it's regressive above that cap. And sales tax is regressive when measured against income.

Household A earns $40,000 and spends $30,000 on taxable goods, paying about $2,063 of sales tax: 5.2 percent of income. Household B earns $200,000 but spends $60,000, paying $4,125: only 2.1 percent.

So the test is always the average rate. Against its own base, sales tax is flat. Against income, it's regressive. Be clear about which base you're using.

Quick check. A tax charges ten percent on the first fifty thousand dollars and five percent above that. What structure is it? Pause and pick.

It's C, regressive. As the base grows, more of it is taxed at five percent, so the average rate falls.

Microteach 1.4 · 1:25The taxes Americans pay

Federal, state and local governments levy income, payroll, sales, property, excise and transfer taxes.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1.

Read the script

The income tax gets the attention, but Americans pay many other taxes. Here's a quick map.

At the federal level there are individual and corporate income taxes, payroll taxes for Social Security and Medicare, excise taxes on things like fuel and alcohol, and transfer taxes on large estates and gifts.

States and local governments add their own income taxes, which Minnesota has, sales and use taxes, and property taxes, which mostly fund schools and local services.

Transfer taxes reach very few people. In 2026 you can give $19,000 a year to each person tax-free, and the estate tax exclusion is fifteen million dollars.

There's also the implicit tax. Municipal bond interest is tax-free, so those bonds pay a lower rate than taxable bonds. The return you give up is an implicit tax, even though no one sends a check to the IRS.

Quick check. A taxable bond yields five percent and a similar municipal bond yields three and a half. What's the implicit tax rate? Pause and pick.

It's B. The muni gives up 1.5 points out of 5, which is thirty percent. An investor in a higher bracket should buy the muni.

Microteach 1.5 · 1:33Where the income tax came from

The Sixteenth Amendment of 1913 made a permanent federal income tax possible.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1. Free reading: Instructor's own material.

Read the script

The federal income tax is younger than you might think. It took a civil war, a Supreme Court defeat and a constitutional amendment.

Congress first taxed incomes in 1861 to pay for the Civil War, then let the tax lapse in 1872. A new income tax in 1894 was struck down by the Supreme Court in Pollock. The Sixteenth Amendment of 1913 settled the question, and paycheck withholding arrived in 1943.

The 1913 tax was a tax on the few. It began at one percent on income above $3,000, at a time when most workers earned far less, with a surtax that took the top rate to seven percent.

The rules have been gathered into a code three times: in 1939, 1954 and 1986. We still work under the 1986 code, amended many times, most recently by the 2025 law called the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

The Second World War turned the income tax into a mass tax, and withholding made it collectible. That's why most Americans now file a return every spring.

Quick check. Which event made a permanent federal income tax constitutional? Pause and pick.

It's C. The Sixteenth Amendment, ratified in 1913, let Congress tax incomes without dividing the tax among the states by population.

Microteach 1.6 · 1:25What makes a good tax?

Judge a tax by sufficiency, equity, certainty, convenience and economy.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 1.

Read the script

Politicians argue about taxes constantly. Economists give us five tests for judging whether a tax is a good one.

A good tax is sufficient: it raises enough revenue. It's equitable, certain, convenient to pay, and economical to run. Four of these tests go back to Adam Smith in 1776.

Equity comes in two kinds. Horizontal equity says people in the same situation pay the same tax. Vertical equity says people with more ability to pay should pay more.

Rate changes also change behavior. Under the income effect, people work more to keep their after-tax income. Under the substitution effect, they work less, because leisure got cheaper. A static forecast ignores both. A dynamic forecast tries to include them.

No tax scores perfectly. A flat tax is simple and certain but weaker on vertical equity. Targeted credits help fairness but add complexity. Policy is a series of trade-offs.

Quick check. Two single filers each earn seventy thousand dollars, but one pays more tax because of where she was born. Which test fails? Pause and pick.

It's B, horizontal equity. Taxpayers in the same situation should pay the same tax.
