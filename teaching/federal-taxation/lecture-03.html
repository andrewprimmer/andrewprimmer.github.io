Microteach 3.1 · 1:21Three ways to plan

Timing, income shifting and conversion are the three basic tax planning strategies.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3.

Read the script

Tax planning isn't about tricks. Almost every legitimate strategy is one of three moves. Here they are.

Timing changes when income or deductions are recognized. Income shifting moves income to a taxpayer in a lower bracket. Conversion changes the type of income, for example from ordinary income to a capital gain taxed at a lower rate.

The goal is to maximize after-tax wealth, not to minimize tax. Turning down a raise would cut your tax bill, but it would make you poorer.

Good planning looks at every party to a transaction, every tax that applies, and every cost, including the non-tax ones like lost flexibility or legal fees.

Paying a property tax bill in December instead of January is timing. Paying fifteen-year-old Leo to help in Nora's business shifts income to him. Holding shares for more than a year converts a short-term gain into a lower-taxed long-term gain.

Quick check. Jake chooses a tax-exempt municipal bond over a taxable bond. Which strategy is it? Pause and pick.

It's C, conversion. He changes the type of income, from taxable interest to tax-exempt interest.

Microteach 3.2 · 1:18Timing and the time value of money

A deduction is worth more the sooner you take it, because of the time value of money.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3.

Read the script

A dollar of tax saved today is worth more than a dollar saved next year. That simple fact drives timing strategies.

Present value is what a future amount is worth today. At a six percent return, a dollar received next year is worth about ninety-four cents now.

Erik, Jake's brother, is in the twenty-four percent bracket. A ten thousand dollar deduction saves him $2,400. Taken this year, that's $2,400 today. Taken next year, it's worth only $2,264 today. Accelerating is worth $136.

So, when tax rates stay the same, accelerate deductions and defer income. Both keep money in your hands longer.

Deferring tax is like an interest-free loan from the government. That's why retirement accounts, which defer tax for decades, are so valuable.

Quick check. Erik can deduct five thousand dollars in December or January, with the same twenty-four percent rate both years. When should he? Pause and pick.

It's A, December. The $1,200 tax saving arrives a year sooner, so it's worth more.

Microteach 3.3 · 1:17When rates change

If your rate will rise, reverse the rule: accelerate income and defer deductions.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3.

Read the script

Accelerate deductions and defer income. That rule flips when your tax rate is about to change.

Suppose Erik expects a promotion that moves him from twenty-four to thirty-two percent next year. Deducting now saves $2,400. Waiting saves $3,200, worth $3,019 today. Deferring the deduction wins by $619.

So if rates are flat or falling, accelerate deductions and defer income. If rates are rising enough, do the opposite: defer deductions and accelerate income.

Your rate changes when your income jumps or drops, when your filing status changes, or when Congress changes the law, as it did in 2017 and again in 2025.

Don't apply the rule blindly. Compare the after-tax present value of each option.

Quick check. Nora expects a much lower rate next year while she cuts back on work. When should she take a three thousand dollar payment from a client? Pause and pick.

It's B, next year. Deferring income into a lower-rate year saves tax and delays it.

Microteach 3.4 · 1:35Shifting income within the family

Shift income to lower-bracket family members through real work, within the kiddie tax rules.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3. Free reading: IRS Pub. 929.

Read the script

Same income, lower bracket. Shifting income to a family member can save tax, if it's done properly.

Fifteen-year-old Leo edits photos for Nora's business. She pays him $8,000, a fair wage for the work. Nora deducts it, saving about $960 of income tax and $1,130 of self-employment tax. Leo's standard deduction covers his wages, so he pays nothing.

It only works if the work is real and the pay is reasonable. A child under eighteen working for a parent's sole proprietorship also owes no Social Security or Medicare tax.

Shifting investment income is harder. Under the kiddie tax, a child's unearned income over $2,700 in 2026 is taxed at the parents' rate. It applies to children under nineteen, and full-time students under twenty-four.

And you can't simply assign your paycheck to your child. Income is taxed to whoever earns it. The Supreme Court said in 1930 that the fruit can't be attributed to a different tree from the one on which it grew.

Quick check. Eight-year-old Ava has five thousand dollars of dividends. How much is taxed at her parents' rate? Pause and pick.

It's C. Unearned income above $2,700 is taxed at the parents' rate: 5,000 minus 2,700 is $2,300.

Microteach 3.5 · 1:18Conversion

Converting ordinary income into capital gains or tax-exempt income lowers the rate applied.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3. Free reading: IRS Pub. 550.

Read the script

Not all income is taxed alike. Conversion changes the character of income to reach a lower rate.

Wages and interest are ordinary income, taxed at ten to thirty-seven percent. Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends get zero, fifteen or twenty percent. Municipal bond interest is tax-exempt.

Erik has a ten thousand dollar gain on shares. Sell after eleven months, and it's a short-term gain taxed at his twenty-four percent rate: $2,400. Wait until he's held them more than a year, and it's taxed at fifteen percent: $1,500.

But conversion has costs. While Erik waits, the share price could fall. Tax-exempt bonds pay lower pre-tax yields. A good plan weighs these against the tax saved.

So the three strategies answer three questions: when is income taxed, who is taxed on it, and what kind of income is it.

Quick check. Which change converts income to a lower rate? Pause and pick.

It's B. Holding the stock over a year turns a short-term gain into a long-term capital gain. The others are timing or shifting.

Microteach 3.6 · 1:16Where planning stops

Business purpose, step transaction and substance over form limit planning; evasion is a crime.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 3.

Read the script

Tax planning has limits. Here are the doctrines courts use to strike down plans that go too far.

First, constructive receipt. A cash-method taxpayer can't defer income by refusing to pick up a check. If the money was available to you without restriction, it's taxed when it was available.

Courts also apply three doctrines. A transaction must have a business purpose beyond saving tax. Steps that only make sense together are collapsed into one. And the substance of a deal, not its legal form, decides how it's taxed.

Tax avoidance, using the law to reduce tax, is legal. Tax evasion, hiding income or inventing deductions, is a crime, punishable by fines and prison.

A useful test: would the transaction make sense if there were no tax? If not, expect the IRS to challenge it.

Quick check. On December thirtieth a client hands Nora a two thousand dollar check. She deposits it in January. When is it income? Pause and pick.

It's A, December. She received it and could have deposited it. Holding the check doesn't defer the income.
