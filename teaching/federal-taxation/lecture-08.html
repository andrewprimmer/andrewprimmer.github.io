Microteach 8.1 · 1:51Computing the regular tax

Apply each bracket's rate to the income inside it; use the tax table below $100,000.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: Form 1040 instructions: tax table and schedules.

Read the script

You've got taxable income. Now turn it into tax. Here's how the 2026 rate schedule works for a joint return.

For a 2026 joint return: ten percent up to $24,800, twelve percent up to $100,800, twenty-two percent up to $211,400, then twenty-four, thirty-two and thirty-five percent, with thirty-seven percent above $768,700.

The Lindqvists' taxable income is $78,505. Ten percent of the first $24,800 is $2,480. Twelve percent of the remaining $53,705 is $6,445. Their regular tax is $8,925.

In practice, taxpayers with taxable income under $100,000 must use the IRS tax table, which taxes the midpoint of each fifty-dollar band. The answer can differ from the schedule by a few dollars.

Joint brackets are exactly double the single ones up to the thirty-two percent bracket, so most couples face no marriage penalty. Couples with one earner usually get a marriage bonus.

Quick check. A married couple has taxable income of a hundred and twenty thousand dollars. What's their regular tax? Pause and pick.

It's B. $11,600 on the first $100,800, plus twenty-two percent of $19,200, which is $4,224. Total $15,824.

Microteach 8.2 · 1:42The kiddie tax and the AMT

The kiddie tax taxes a child's unearned income over $2,700 at the parents' rate; the AMT is a parallel tax with a broader base.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: Form 8615; Form 6251.

Read the script

Two add-on taxes can raise the regular tax: the kiddie tax for children, and the alternative minimum tax for some high earners.

Ava, eight, has $5,000 of dividends from shares her grandmother gave her. The first $1,350 is covered by her standard deduction. The next $1,350 is taxed at her own rate. The last $2,300, above $2,700, is taxed at her parents' rate.

The alternative minimum tax is a second, parallel calculation. It disallows some deductions and taxes some exempt income, then applies rates of twenty-six and twenty-eight percent above an exemption. If it's higher than the regular tax, you pay the difference.

For 2026 the exemption is $90,100, or $140,200 for a joint return. It phases out above $500,000 of income, or a million dollars joint, at fifty cents on the dollar.

Common triggers are large state and local tax deductions, which the AMT adds back, the bargain element on incentive stock options, and interest on certain private activity bonds. With higher exemptions since 2018, few families pay it.

Quick check. Leo, fifteen, has two thousand dollars of interest income and nothing else. Does the kiddie tax apply? Pause and pick.

It's C. The kiddie tax only reaches unearned income above $2,700.

Microteach 8.3 · 1:50Payroll and self-employment taxes

Employees and employers each pay 7.65%; the self-employed pay 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: Schedule SE instructions.

Read the script

Income tax is only half the story. Social Security and Medicare taxes take a big bite too, especially from the self-employed.

On wages, the employee pays six point two percent Social Security tax on the first $184,500, and one point four five percent Medicare tax on everything. The employer matches both. Employees also pay an extra point nine percent Medicare tax on wages over $200,000.

The self-employed pay both halves, as self-employment tax. Nora's net profit is $30,000. Multiply by ninety-two point three five percent to get $27,705, then by fifteen point three percent: $4,239. Half of it, $2,119, is a deduction for AGI.

Why ninety-two point three five percent? An employee isn't taxed on the employer's half of FICA. Multiplying by one minus half of fifteen point three percent mimics that.

If you have both wages and self-employment income, wages use up the $184,500 Social Security base first. Self-employment income only pays the twelve point four percent on whatever room is left.

Quick check. A freelancer has fifty thousand dollars of net profit. What's her self-employment tax? Pause and pick.

It's B. $50,000 times ninety-two point three five percent is $46,175. Times fifteen point three percent is about $7,065.

Microteach 8.4 · 1:38Child tax credit and dependent care credit

The child tax credit is $2,200 per child under 17; the dependent care credit pays 20% to 50% of care costs.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: Schedule 8812; Form 2441.

Read the script

Two credits matter to almost every working family: the child tax credit and the child and dependent care credit.

First, a distinction. A nonrefundable credit can reduce your tax to zero but no further. A refundable credit is paid to you even if you owe no tax.

The child tax credit is $2,200 for each qualifying child under seventeen with a Social Security number. Up to $1,700 per child is refundable. Other dependents, like Ruth, earn a $500 credit. It phases out above $400,000 of income on a joint return, or $200,000 otherwise.

The dependent care credit covers care for a child under thirteen so that you can work. From 2026 it's fifty percent of up to $3,000 of costs for one child, or $6,000 for two, for the lowest incomes, falling to thirty-five and then twenty percent as income rises.

Suppose the Lindqvists pay $3,000 for Ava's after-school care while both parents work. At their AGI the rate is thirty-five percent, so the credit is $1,050. Before 2026 it would have been twenty percent, or $600.

Quick check. Which of these earns the $2,200 child tax credit? Pause and pick.

It's A. Leo is under seventeen. Sam, the seventeen-year-old and Ruth can each earn the $500 other dependent credit instead.

Microteach 8.5 · 1:39Education credits

The American opportunity credit gives up to $2,500 for each of the first four years; the lifetime learning credit 20% of up to $10,000.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: IRS Pub. 970.

Read the script

College costs earn two credits. Here's how they compare, and how fast they disappear as income rises.

The American opportunity credit gives up to $2,500 per student for the first four years of college, and forty percent is refundable. The lifetime learning credit is twenty percent of up to $10,000 of costs per return, for any year of study, but isn't refundable.

The American opportunity credit is one hundred percent of the first $2,000 of tuition, fees and course materials, plus twenty-five percent of the next $2,000: a maximum of $2,500.

Both credits phase out between $80,000 and $90,000 of modified AGI, or $160,000 to $180,000 on a joint return. These ranges aren't indexed.

After his scholarship, Maria pays $4,000 of Sam's tuition. That earns the full $2,500 before the phase-out. But her modified AGI is $85,000, halfway through the range, so she keeps half: $1,250.

Quick check. Jake takes a graduate agronomy course costing three thousand dollars. Which credit fits? Pause and pick.

It's B. Graduate study isn't in the first four years, so it's the lifetime learning credit: twenty percent of $3,000, or $600.

Microteach 8.6 · 1:47The EITC and paying during the year

The earned income credit supports low earners; withholding and estimated payments must reach a safe harbor.

Reading: McGraw Hill's Taxation of Individuals, Chapter 8. Free reading: IRS Pub. 596; Form 1040-ES.

Read the script

One credit supports millions of low-income workers. And everyone has to pay their tax during the year, not just in April.

The earned income tax credit is refundable. It rises with earnings, levels off, then phases out as income grows. It's larger for families with children: up to $8,231 in 2026 with three or more qualifying children.

The income tax is pay-as-you-go. Employees pay through withholding. The self-employed, like Nora, make quarterly estimated payments, due April fifteenth, June fifteenth, September fifteenth and January fifteenth.

To avoid an underpayment penalty, pay at least ninety percent of this year's tax, or one hundred percent of last year's, rising to one hundred and ten percent if last year's AGI was over $150,000. No penalty applies if you owe less than $1,000 when you file.

The Lindqvists' total tax last year was $8,400. Jake's withholding will cover $6,000, so Nora needs $2,400 of estimates, $600 a quarter, to be safe, whatever her business earns this year.

Quick check. A couple's 2025 tax was twenty thousand dollars, on AGI of a hundred and eighty thousand. What 2026 payments guarantee no penalty? Pause and pick.

It's C. Their prior-year AGI was over $150,000, so the safe harbor is one hundred and ten percent of $20,000: $22,000.
