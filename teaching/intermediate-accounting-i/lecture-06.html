Microteach 6.1 · 1:15Why accountants need time value of money

Many accounting measurements are present values: notes, bonds, leases, pensions and impairments.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar next year. In intermediate accounting, that idea is everywhere.

Present values measure notes with low or no stated interest, bonds, leases, pension obligations, impairment losses and many fair values. Master this lecture and the second half of the course is much easier.

Simple interest is earned on the principal only: ten thousand dollars at eight percent for three years earns $2,400. Compound interest is earned on principal plus interest already earned: $2,597. Accounting uses compound interest.

Every problem has four variables: an amount, an interest rate per period, a number of periods, and a future or present value. Know three and you can find the fourth.

Match the rate to the period. Eight percent a year compounded quarterly means two percent per period, with four periods each year.

Quick check. Twelve percent a year compounded monthly for two years means what rate and how many periods? Pause and pick.

It's B, one percent for twenty-four periods.

Microteach 6.2 · 1:21Future value of a single sum

FV = PV × (1 + i)ⁿ.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

Bluestem puts $100,000 aside today to fund a new mold in five years. How much will it have?

The future value of a single sum is the present amount times one plus the interest rate, raised to the number of periods. Tables give the factor directly.

At six percent compounded annually for five years, the factor is one point three three eight two three. One hundred thousand dollars grows to $133,823.

Year by year, interest earns interest: $106,000, $112,360, and so on to $133,823. Compound interest adds $33,823, versus $30,000 with simple interest.

In a spreadsheet, the FV function gives the same answer. Enter the present value as a negative, because it's money going out.

Quick check. Fifty thousand dollars invested for three years at ten percent compounded annually grows to how much? Pause and pick.

It's B, $66,550: fifty thousand times one point three three one.

Microteach 6.3 · 1:23Present value of a single sum

PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ: discounting is the reverse of compounding.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

Flip the question. Bluestem needs $500,000 in four years for a warehouse expansion. How much must it invest today?

The present value is the future amount divided by one plus the rate, raised to the number of periods. Discounting is just compounding in reverse.

At five percent for four years, the present value factor is point eight two two seven. Bluestem must invest $411,351 today.

The higher the rate, or the longer the wait, the smaller the present value. At eight percent, Bluestem would need only $367,515 today.

Accounting use: if a dealer gives Bluestem a $500,000 note due in four years with no interest, the sale isn't really $500,000. At a five percent market rate, the revenue is $411,351, and the rest is interest earned over four years.

Quick check. What's the present value of twenty thousand dollars due in two years at ten percent? Pause and pick.

It's B, $16,529: twenty thousand divided by one point two one.

Microteach 6.4 · 1:02Solving for the rate or the number of periods

Rearrange the factor: factor = FV ÷ PV, then find the rate or periods in the table.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

Sometimes you know the amounts and need the rate or the time.

An investment of $200,000 grows to $300,000 in six years. The required future value factor is three hundred divided by two hundred, one point five. In the six-period row, seven percent gives one point five zero zero seven three. The rate is about seven percent.

How long does money take to double at eight percent? Look for a factor of two in the eight percent column: nine periods gives one point nine nine nine.

A handy check is the rule of seventy-two: divide seventy-two by the rate. Seventy-two divided by eight is nine years.

In a spreadsheet, the RATE and NPER functions solve these exactly.

Quick check. Using the rule of seventy-two, how long does money take to double at six percent? Pause and pick.

It's B, about twelve years.

Microteach 6.5 · 1:16Future value of an annuity

An annuity is equal payments at equal intervals; annuity due payments come at the start of each period.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

Instead of one deposit, Bluestem will put $50,000 into a fund every year for five years.

An annuity is a series of equal payments at equal intervals. In an ordinary annuity, payments come at the end of each period, like loan payments. In an annuity due, they come at the start, like rent.

For an ordinary annuity of $50,000 at the end of each year for five years at six percent, the factor is five point six three seven. The fund grows to $281,855: $250,000 deposited plus $31,855 of interest.

If Bluestem deposits at the start of each year, every payment earns one more period of interest. Multiply the ordinary factor by one point zero six: the fund grows to $298,766.

The best habit in this topic: draw a timeline and mark exactly when each payment falls before you pick a table.

Quick check. Which is usually an annuity due? Pause and pick.

It's B: rent paid in advance.

Microteach 6.6 · 1:27Present value of an annuity

PV of an annuity values a stream of equal payments today; it measures loans, leases and bond interest.

Reading: Intermediate Accounting, Chapter 6.

Read the script

A supplier offers Bluestem a new paint line for five annual payments of $40,000. What does it really cost?

At an eight percent market rate, the present value factor for five end-of-year payments is three point nine nine two seven. The cost today is $159,708, not $200,000.

Bluestem records the equipment and the note at $159,708. The extra $40,292 it pays over five years is interest expense, recognized as time passes.

If the first payment were due today, it's an annuity due. Multiply the factor by one point zero eight: the present value is $172,485. Earlier payments cost more in today's dollars.

In a spreadsheet, PV with the rate, periods and payment gives the ordinary annuity. Add a one as the type argument for an annuity due.

Quick check. What's the present value of ten thousand dollars a year for three years at ten percent, an ordinary annuity with factor two point four eight six eight five? Pause and pick.

It's B, $24,869.
