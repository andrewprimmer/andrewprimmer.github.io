Teaching · Distance learning

ACCT 416 Intermediate Accounting I

Accounting standards, the conceptual framework, the financial statements, time value of money, cash and receivables, inventories and property, plant and equipment, in 14 lectures and 84 two-minute videos, with a slide deck and an interactive exercise set for every lecture.

Required textbook

Intermediate Accounting, 19th edition

Donald E. Kieso, Jerry J. Weygandt, Terry D. Warfield and Laura D. Wiley. Wiley, 2025. WileyPLUS optional.

The standard US intermediate accounting text, used in more programs than any other and closely aligned with the CPA exam. ACCT 416 and ACCT 312 share this one book and split its chapters between them with no overlap, so students buy it once for both courses.

ACCT 416 reads Chapters 1–11. ACCT 312 Intermediate Accounting II reads Chapters 12–18, 22 and 23.

An intensive study of financial accounting and reporting: accounting standards and the conceptual framework, the income statement and balance sheet, time value of money, cash and receivables, inventories, and the acquisition, depreciation and disposition of property, plant and equipment. Prerequisite: ACCT 212 and ECON 201 or 202. One fictional Marshall, Minnesota company runs through the course: Bluestem Outdoor, a maker of kayaks, tents and camping gear, with its controller Sam Nygaard, as it prepares for a bank loan and, eventually, a public listing. ACCT 312 Intermediate Accounting II uses the same textbook and follows Bluestem after it goes public.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook chapter for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set (112 questions in all), then the textbook's end-of-chapter problems.
Every lecture has a textbook chapter, six videos, a two-hour slide deck and an interactive exercise set. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Lectures

Part one · Foundations

  1. L1Financial accounting and accounting standardsWhy financial reporting matters for capital allocation, the objective of financial reporting, the SEC, FASB and PCAOB, how standards are set, the Codification, IFRS, and ethics. Read: Chapter 1.Week 1
  2. L2The conceptual framework for financial reportingThe purpose of the conceptual framework, qualitative characteristics of useful information, the elements of financial statements, the basic assumptions, measurement and recognition principles, and the cost constraint. Read: Chapter 2.Week 2
  3. L3The accounting information system and adjusting entriesThe accounting cycle, adjusting entries for prepayments and accruals, depreciation, closing entries, reversing entries, and converting cash-basis records to accrual. Read: Chapter 3.Week 3
  4. L4The income statement and related informationUsefulness and limits of the income statement, quality of earnings, the multiple-step format, discontinued operations, unusual items and intraperiod tax allocation, earnings per share, retained earnings and comprehensive income. Read: Chapter 4.Week 4
  5. L5The balance sheet, cash flows and disclosureUsefulness and limits of the balance sheet, classification of assets, liabilities and equity, liquidity and solvency ratios, the purpose and sections of the statement of cash flows, free cash flow, and disclosure techniques. Read: Chapter 5.Week 5

Part two · Time value of money

  1. L6Time value of money: single sums and annuitiesWhy accountants need time value of money, simple and compound interest, future and present values of single sums, solving for rates and periods, and future and present values of ordinary annuities and annuities due. Read: Chapter 6.Week 6
  2. L7Time value of money: deferred annuities, bonds and measurementDeferred annuities, valuing long-term bonds, the effective-interest method, solving for loan payments and amortization schedules, and present value measurement with expected cash flows. Read: Chapter 6.Week 7

Part three · Cash, receivables and inventory

  1. L8Cash and receivablesWhat counts as cash, recognizing accounts receivable with discounts, estimating credit losses with an aging schedule, write-offs and recoveries, notes receivable at present value, transfers of receivables, and receivables turnover. Read: Chapter 7.Week 9
  2. L9Inventories: classification, systems and what to includeInventory classifications for manufacturers and merchandisers, perpetual and periodic systems, goods in transit and on consignment, which costs to include, and the effects of inventory errors. Read: Chapter 8.Week 10
  3. L10Inventory cost flow assumptionsSpecific identification, average cost, FIFO and LIFO under periodic and perpetual systems, the LIFO reserve, LIFO liquidation, dollar-value LIFO, and choosing a method. Read: Chapter 8.Week 11
  4. L11Inventories: valuation and estimationLower of cost and net realizable value, lower of cost or market for LIFO and retail, recording write-downs and purchase commitment losses, the gross profit method, the retail inventory method, and inventory analysis. Read: Chapter 9.Week 12

Part four · Property, plant and equipment

  1. L12Property, plant and equipment: acquisitionMeasuring the cost of equipment, land and buildings, self-constructed assets and interest capitalization, lump-sum purchases and other acquisitions, nonmonetary exchanges, and costs after acquisition. Read: Chapter 10.Week 13
  2. L13Depreciation methods and revisionsDepreciation as cost allocation, the activity, straight-line, sum-of-the-years'-digits and declining-balance methods, partial periods, group and composite methods, tax depreciation, and revisions of estimates. Read: Chapter 11.Week 14
  3. L14Impairments, depletion and disposalsImpairment of assets held for use and held for disposal, depletion of natural resources, disposals and involuntary conversions, presentation and analysis of PP&E, and a recap of the course. Read: Chapters 10 and 11.Week 15
