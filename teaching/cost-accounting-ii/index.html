Teaching · Distance learning

ACCT 341 Cost Accounting II

Designing and using cost and managerial accounting systems for planning, performance evaluation and decision-making, in 14 lectures and 84 two-minute videos, with a slide deck and an interactive exercise set for every lecture.

Required textbook

Horngren's Cost Accounting, 18th edition

Srikant M. Datar and Madhav V. Rajan. Pearson, 2025 (© 2026). eTextbook ISBN 9780135337936; MyLab Accounting optional.

The standard cost accounting text in the US, built around the theme of different costs for different purposes. ACCT 341 and ACCT 413 share this one book and split its 24 chapters between them with no overlap: if you took ACCT 413, you already own it.

ACCT 341 reads Chapters 3, 6, 9–15 and 20–24. ACCT 413 Cost Accounting I reads the other ten.

An advanced study of the design, development and use of cost and managerial accounting systems for planning, performance evaluation and analysis in management decision-making: cost behavior and prediction, data analytics, CVP, budgeting, relevant costs, pricing, capital budgeting, customer profitability, the balanced scorecard, quality and time, inventory management, transfer pricing and performance measurement. Prerequisite: MGMT 300 and ACCT 340 or ACCT 413. The course builds on ACCT 413 Cost Accounting I. One fictional Marshall, Minnesota company runs through it: Tallgrass Cycles, an e-bike maker with a Trail bike and a Cargo bike, its CFO Ana Vang, and its dealers Prairie Pedals, Lakes Cycle and Big Sky Bikes.

How to study it

  1. Work through the lectures in order. Read the textbook chapter for each lecture.
  2. Watch the six microteaches. Each is under two minutes, made to be watched on a phone.
  3. When a video asks a quick-check question, pause it and answer before the reveal.
  4. Finish each lecture with its exercise set (112 questions in all), then the textbook's end-of-chapter problems.
Every lecture has a textbook chapter, six videos, a two-hour slide deck and an interactive exercise set. The videos are narrated with a synthetic voice.

Lectures

Part one · Cost behavior and prediction

  1. L1Determining how costs behaveLinear cost functions and the relevant range, methods of estimating costs, the high-low method, regression analysis, choosing cost drivers, and nonlinear costs such as learning curves. Read: Chapter 10.Week 1
  2. L2Data analytic thinking and predictionA framework for data analytics, classification with decision trees, measuring purity, overfitting and validation, evaluating predictions with a confusion matrix, and turning predictions into decisions. Read: Chapter 11.Week 2

Part two · Planning

  1. L3Cost–volume–profit analysisContribution margin, the breakeven point, target income before and after tax, using CVP for decisions and sensitivity analysis, cost structure and operating leverage, and multiple products with a sales mix. Read: Chapter 3.Week 3
  2. L4The master budgetWhy companies budget, the order of the operating budget, the revenue and production budgets, direct materials usage and purchases, the cash budget, and the behavioral side of budgeting. Read: Chapter 6.Week 4
  3. L5Responsibility accounting and sales variancesResponsibility centers, controllability and performance reports, the sales-volume variance, its split into sales-mix and sales-quantity variances, and market-share and market-size variances. Read: Chapters 6 and 15.Week 5
  4. L6Inventory costing and capacity analysisVariable and absorption costing, why their incomes differ, the incentive to overproduce, throughput costing, four capacity levels for the fixed overhead rate, and the downward demand spiral. Read: Chapter 9.Week 6

Part three · Decisions

  1. L7Decision making and relevant informationWhat makes information relevant, special orders, make-or-buy and opportunity costs, product mix under a constraint, keeping or dropping a product line, and equipment replacement with sunk costs. Read: Chapter 12.Week 7
  2. L8Pricing decisions and cost managementCustomers, competitors and costs as influences on price, target pricing and target costing, value engineering, cost-plus pricing, life-cycle budgeting, and price discrimination and the law. Read: Chapter 14.Week 9
  3. L9Cost allocation and customer-profitability analysisThe purposes and criteria of cost allocation, allocating corporate costs to divisions, analyzing customer revenues and price discounts, the customer cost hierarchy, customer-profitability profiles, and acting on them. Read: Chapter 15.Week 10

Part four · Performance and control

  1. L10Strategy, the balanced scorecard and strategic profitability analysisDifferentiation and cost leadership, the four perspectives of the balanced scorecard, building a scorecard and strategy map, splitting operating income into growth, price-recovery and productivity, engineered and discretionary costs, and scorecard pitfalls. Read: Chapter 13.Week 11
  2. L11Quality and time as competitive toolsDesign and conformance quality, the four costs of quality, tools for finding quality problems, relevant costs of quality decisions, customer-response time and queues, and the theory of constraints. Read: Chapter 20.Week 12
  3. L12Inventory management, just-in-time and backflush costingThe costs of holding inventory, the economic order quantity, reorder points and safety stock, the cost of prediction errors, just-in-time purchasing and production, and backflush costing. Read: Chapter 21.Week 13
  4. L13Capital budgeting and cost analysisThe stages of capital budgeting, net present value, internal rate of return, payback and accrual accounting rate of return, income taxes and relevant cash flows, and post-investment audits. Read: Chapter 22.Week 14
  5. L14Transfer pricing and performance measurementManagement control systems, market-based, cost-based and negotiated transfer prices, the minimum transfer price rule, multinational taxes, ROI, residual income and EVA, and a recap of the course. Read: Chapters 23 and 24.Week 15
